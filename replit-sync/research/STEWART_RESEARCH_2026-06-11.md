## 3. Named Models and Key Formulas

### 3.1 The Synestia Model and the Corotation Limit (CoRoL)

**Source papers:** [P8] Lock & Stewart 2017 (JGR:P), [P6] Lock et al. 2018 (JGR:P)  
**arXiv:** [1705.07858](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07858), [1802.10223](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10223)

#### What is a synestia?
A **synestia** is a post-giant-impact planetary structure in which a hot, partially or fully vaporized body rotates so rapidly that its outermost equatorial material exceeds the local Keplerian orbital velocity. The body can no longer be a simple oblate spheroid; instead it forms a connected structure with a dense, corotating inner region (like a proto-planet) and a continuous disk-like outer region in Keplerian-ish orbits — resembling a donut or inflated red blood cell, entirely in vapor/mixed phase with no discrete surface.

#### The Corotation Limit (CoRoL)

The CoRoL is defined as the thermal state and angular momentum \(L\) at which the rotational velocity at the equator of a corotating body exactly equals the local Keplerian orbital velocity:

\[
\omega_{\rm eq}(L, M, S) = \sqrt{\frac{G M(r)}{r^3}}\Big|_{r=R_{\rm eq}}
\]

where \(\omega_{\rm eq}\) is the angular velocity at the equator, \(M(r)\) the enclosed mass at radius \(r\), and \(R_{\rm eq}\) the equatorial radius. The angular velocity of a corotating body is:

\[
\omega = \frac{L}{I(L, M, S, Z)}
\]

where \(I\) is the moment of inertia — which itself depends on \(L\) (rotation), \(M\) (total mass), \(S\) (specific entropy of the outer layers, i.e. thermal state), and \(Z\) (compositional layering / core mass fraction). The CoRoL is therefore a **surface** in (L, M, S, Z) parameter space rather than a single threshold number. Lock & Stewart computed this surface using their HERCULES code, which solves planetary equilibrium structure as a series of overlapping constant-density spheroids.

Operationally: **a body exceeds the CoRoL and forms a synestia if and only if:**
\[
L > L_{\rm CoRoL}(M, S_{\rm outer}, Z)
\]
For an Earth-mass body with \(S_{\rm outer} \approx 6\;\text{kJ\,K}^{-1}\text{kg}^{-1}\) (typical post-impact outer entropy), the CoRoL corresponds to an angular momentum of roughly \(2\text{--}3\,L_{\rm EM}\) (where \(L_{\rm EM} = 3.5 \times 10^{34}\;\text{g\,cm}^2\text{s}^{-1}\) is the present Earth–Moon angular momentum). The inner corotating region has:
\[
\omega_{\rm corot} = \frac{L}{I} = \text{const across the inner region}
\]
and the disk-like outer region has material in Keplerian-ish orbits:
\[
\Omega(r) \approx \sqrt{\frac{G M_{\rm enc}}{r^3}}
\]
The **transition radius** where these two solutions match is the inner boundary of the disk-like region.

#### Synestia Surface Temperature Buffer
The surface (photospheric) temperature of a synestia is constrained by the silicate boiling point:
\[
T_{\rm surface} \approx 2300\;\text{K} \quad \text{(~1 bar boiling point of silicate)}
\]
This fixes the radiative cooling rate and thereby the synestia's lifetime (~100 years for Earth-mass).

#### Angular Momentum Budget
System angular momentum is conserved during the giant impact:
\[
L_{\rm sys} = L_{\rm proto-Earth,\,spin} + L_{\rm impactor,\,orbital} + L_{\rm impactor,\,spin}
\]
For the Ćuk & Stewart 2012 scenario (fast-spinning Earth):
- Pre-impact Earth spin period: **T ≈ 2–3 hours** (near breakup at ~2 hr)
- Post-impact total \(L\): **1.9–2.8 \(L_{\rm EM}\)**, requiring removal of ~50% via evection resonance
- Disk mass: **1.8–5.5 \(M_{\rm Moon}\)** with iron fraction \(M_{\rm Fe}/M_D < 0.06\)

For the Lock et al. 2018 synestia scenario:
- \(L_{\rm sys} \gtrsim 2\,L_{\rm EM}\) to form a Moon-forming synestia
- Synestia pressure at proto-lunar orbit: **tens of bars** (≈30–60 bar)
- Temperature at Moon-forming depth: **~4000–6000 K** (internal vapor); surface ~2300 K

### 3.2 Ćuk & Stewart 2012: Fast-Spinning Earth / Evection Resonance Model

**Source paper:** [P9] Ćuk & Stewart, *Science* **338**, 1047–1052, 2012.  
DOI: [10.1126/science.1225542](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225542)

#### Core Proposition
A **sub-Mars-mass impactor** (mass ratio \(\gamma = M_{\rm imp}/M_T \approx 0.4\text{–}0.5\)) hits a **fast-spinning proto-Earth** (\(T_{\rm Earth} \approx 2.3\text{–}2.7\) hr, \(\approx 30\%\) of breakup speed). This:
1. Ejects an iron-poor, Earth-mantle-composition disk (50–90% vapor)
2. Generates \(\delta f_T \approx 0\%\) — disk silicate composition nearly identical to Earth's mantle
3. Produces total \(L_{\rm sys} \approx 1.9\text{–}2.8\,L_{\rm EM}\)

#### The Evection Resonance
After Moon formation, the excess \(L\) is removed via the **solar evection resonance** — a commensurability between the Moon's apsidal precession period and one year:
\[
\dot{\varpi}_{\rm Moon} = n_{\odot}
\]
where \(\dot{\varpi}\) is the Moon's apsidal precession rate and \(n_{\odot} = 2\pi/(1\;\text{yr})\) is the Sun's mean motion. Tidal coupling during this resonance transfers angular momentum from the Earth–Moon orbit to the Sun, reducing system \(L\) by a factor of ~2 to reach the present-day value \(L_{\rm EM}\).

The key tidal constraint: the ratio of the Moon's to Earth's tidal dissipation factors must satisfy a specific range:
\[
\frac{(k_2/Q)_{\rm Moon}}{(k_2/Q)_{\oplus}} \in \text{narrow range}
\]

#### SPH Impact Parameters (Table 1, Ćuk & Stewart 2012)
| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Total mass | \(M_T = 1.04\,M_\oplus\) |
| Impactor mass ratio | \(\gamma = 0.40\text{–}0.45\) |
| Impact parameter | \(b = 0.35\text{–}0.70\) |
| Impact velocity | \(v_{\rm imp}/v_{\rm esc} = 1.0\text{–}1.6\) |
| Relative \(v_\infty\) | \(0\text{–}11\;\text{km/s}\) |
| Post-impact \(L_F/L_{\rm EM}\) | \(1.77\text{–}2.71\) |
| Moon mass | \(M_M/M_L = 0.96\text{–}4.39\) |
| Disk Fe fraction | \(< 6\%\) |
| Disk vapor fraction | \(50\text{–}90\%\) |

The impact velocity–escape velocity relation:
\[
v_{\rm imp}^2 = v_{\rm esc}^2 + v_\infty^2
\]
where \(v_{\rm esc} = \sqrt{2G(M_{\rm target}+M_{\rm imp})/(R_{\rm target}+R_{\rm imp})}\).

### 3.3 Equations of State: Hugoniot Relations and M-ANEOS

**Source papers:** [P14] Stewart et al. 2020/arXiv 2019, [P16] Root et al. 2018, [P17] Kraus et al. 2012, [P18] Kraus et al. 2015  
**M-ANEOS GitHub:** [isale-code/M-ANEOS](https://github.com/isale-code/M-ANEOS)

#### The Rankine–Hugoniot Relations
The **Hugoniot** is the locus of thermodynamic states reachable by a single shock. For a planar shock propagating at velocity \(U_s\) into undisturbed material (density \(\rho_0\), pressure \(P_0\), internal energy \(E_0\)), with particle velocity \(u_p\) behind the shock:

**Mass conservation:**
\[
\rho_0 U_s = \rho_1 (U_s - u_p)
\]

**Momentum conservation:**
\[
P_1 - P_0 = \rho_0 U_s u_p
\]

**Energy conservation:**
\[
E_1 - E_0 = \frac{1}{2}(P_1 + P_0)(V_0 - V_1)
\]
where \(V = 1/\rho\). These three relations are supplemented by the **empirical linear Hugoniot relation** (valid for most solids up to ~1000 GPa):
\[
U_s = c_0 + s\,u_p
\]
where \(c_0\) is the bulk sound speed and \(s\) is a dimensionless material constant (~1.2–1.8 for silicates).

**For quartz (α-SiO₂)** — measured fit from [P17] Kraus et al. 2012:
\[
U_s = -295.7 + 2.139\,u_p - 4.012 \times 10^{-5}\,u_p^2 \quad \text{[m/s]}
\]
(nonlinear fit valid across melting/vaporization region, both \(U_s\) and \(u_p\) in m/s)

#### Entropy Along the Hugoniot
Entropy \(S\) is computed by integrating the first law:
\[
dS = \frac{dE + P\,dV}{T}
\]
For the shock path, the specific entropy increase from initial state \((P_0, V_0, T_0)\) to shocked state \((P_H, V_H, T_H)\) is:
\[
S_H = S_0 + \int_{(P_0,V_0)}^{(P_H,V_H)} \frac{dE + P\,dV}{T}
\]
From [P17], the **absolute entropy of quartz along its Hugoniot** as a polynomial in shock pressure \(P\) (GPa), valid for \(P = 113\text{–}800\) GPa:
\[
S_{H,\rm Qtz}(P) = 1793 + 22.40\,P - 0.04260\,P^2 + 4.319\times10^{-5}\,P^3 - 1.742\times10^{-8}\,P^4 \quad \text{[J\,kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\text{]}
\]
Uncertainty:
\[
\delta S_{H,\rm Qtz}(P) = 127.3 + 0.328\,P - 1.93\times10^{-4}\,P^2 \quad \text{[J\,kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\text{]}
\]
And for fused silica (FQ) at \(P = 70\text{–}110\) GPa:
\[
S_{H,\rm FQ}(P) = 2294 + 26.82\,P - 0.0723\,P^2
\]

#### Shock Vaporization Criteria for SiO₂ (from [P17] Table 6)
Upon isentropic **release** from the shocked state to 1 bar (ambient pressure \(10^5\) Pa):

| Criterion | Entropy threshold \(S_{\rm IV}\) | Entropy for complete vaporization \(S_{\rm CV}\) | Onset pressure \(P_{\rm IV}\) | Pressure for 50% vapor \(P_{50\%}\) | Complete vaporization pressure \(P_{\rm CV}\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| This study (298 K α-quartz, 1 bar release) | 3552 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ | 7254 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ | 75 ± 5 GPa | 258 ± 25 GPa | 715 ± 100 GPa |
| M-ANEOS (Melosh 2007) | 3443 | 7240 | 95 GPa | 380 GPa | 1650 GPa |

These values are substantially lower than the previous M-ANEOS estimate, meaning **silicate vapor production in giant impacts is much more efficient than previously modeled**.

**Critical new SiO₂ thermodynamic parameters (from [P17]):**
- Critical point: \(T_{\rm CP} = 5130\;\text{K}\), \(\rho_{\rm CP} = 0.508\;\text{g\,cm}^{-3}\), \(P_{\rm CP} = 0.13\;\text{GPa}\), \(S_{\rm CP} = 5150\;\text{J\,kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
- Entropy of SiO₂ vapor at boiling point (3177 K): \(7254 \pm 8\;\text{J\,kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\)
- Enthalpy of vaporization: \(11770 \pm 950\;\text{kJ\,kg}^{-1}\)

#### Iron Vaporization Threshold (from [P18] Kraus et al. 2015)
Using the Sandia Z-machine, **new experimental measurement**:
\[
P_{\rm vap,\,Fe} = 507^{+65}_{-85}\;\text{GPa}
\]
vs. prior theoretical estimate of ~887 GPa. This ~40% reduction has major implications: iron cores of differentiated planetesimals **vaporize in typical late-accretion impacts**, dispersing iron as rain rather than diapirs.

#### M-ANEOS: Helmholtz Free Energy EOS Framework
The ANEOS family (Thompson & Lauson 1972; Melosh 2007; **Stewart et al. 2019/2020**) expresses the Helmholtz free energy as:
\[
F(\rho, T) = F_{\rm cold}(\rho) + F_{\rm thermal}(\rho, T) + F_{\rm electronic}(\rho, T)
\]

- **Cold term** \(F_{\rm cold}\): describes atomic/electronic interactions along the cold compression curve (zero temperature)
- **Thermal term** \(F_{\rm thermal}\): uses a Debye/Grüneisen model
  - Original ANEOS: hardcoded Dulong-Petit limit — \(C_V \to 3Nk_B\) (atoms)
  - **Stewart et al. modification**: user-defined \(C_V^{\rm lim}\) allowing a reduced heat capacity for the liquid/supercritical phase, giving:
    \[
    F_{\rm thermal} = -Nk_BT\left[\frac{3}{2}\ln\left(\frac{T}{T_{\rm Debye}}\right) + \cdots\right] \quad \text{with } C_V \leq C_V^{\rm lim}
\]
- **Electronic term** \(F_{\rm electron}\): Ionization at high \(T\), low \(\rho\)

The pressure and entropy are derived as:
\[
P = -\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial V}\right)_T, \quad S = -\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}\right)_V
\]
M-ANEOS also uses **molecular clusters** (rather than monatomic gas) in the vapor phase, and a **Mie-type potential** in the expanded solid (Melosh 2007 modification). Stewart et al.'s 2019 update improves the forsterite (Mg₂SiO₄) model to fit the liquid–vapor phase boundary and Hugoniot more accurately.

### 3.4 Leinhardt–Stewart (LS12) Collision Scaling Laws

**Source papers:** [P12] Leinhardt & Stewart 2012 (ApJ **745**, 79); [P11] Stewart & Leinhardt 2012 (ApJ **751**, 32)  
**arXiv:** [1106.6084](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.6084), [1109.4588](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.4588)

These scaling laws are the standard analytic collision model used in planet formation N-body codes. They define five collision outcome regimes based on mass ratio \(\gamma = M_{\rm imp}/M_{\rm target}\), impact parameter \(b\), and specific impact energy \(Q_R\):

1. **Cratering** — target largely intact, projectile mostly destroyed
2. **Merging** — nearly perfect accretion
3. **Partial accretion** — target grows, some ejecta
4. **Hit-and-run** — impactor bounces off, both bodies survive
5. **Disruption / super-catastrophic disruption** — both bodies shattered

The **catastrophic disruption criterion** \(Q^*_{RD}\) (energy to disperse half the total mass) in the gravity regime:
\[
Q^*_{RD} = c^* \frac{4}{5}\pi \rho_1 G R_{C1}^2 \left[\frac{(1+\gamma)^2}{4\gamma}\right]^{-1+2/(3\bar{\mu})}
\]
where \(\rho_1\) is the density of the target, \(R_{C1}\) is the radius of a sphere with the target's mass, \(\gamma = M_{\rm imp}/M_{\rm target}\), \(\bar{\mu}\) is the reduced mass exponent (~0.36 for gravity-dominated bodies), and \(c^*\) is a dimensionless scaling constant (~1.9 for rubble piles).

The **largest remnant mass** in the universal-law branch:
\[
\frac{M_{\rm lr}}{M_{\rm tot}} = -0.5\left(\frac{Q_R}{Q^*_{RD}} - 1\right) + 0.5 \quad \text{for} \quad Q_R \leq 2Q^*_{RD}
\]
\[
\frac{M_{\rm lr}}{M_{\rm tot}} = \eta\left(\frac{Q_R}{Q^*_{RD}}\right)^{-\eta'} \quad \text{for super-catastrophic branch}
\]
(with empirical constants fitted to simulations).

### 3.5 IVANS Model — Planetesimal Impact Vapor Plumes (2025)

**Source paper:** [P1] Stewart et al., PSJ in press, 2025. arXiv: [2503.05636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05636)

Develops a physical model for chondrule formation from nebular shocks driven by **impact-generated silicate vapor plumes**. The framework integrates:
- Shock-induced vaporization thresholds from [P17] and [P18]
- Nebular gas dynamics from 1D/2D/3D shock hydrodynamics
- Scaling laws for the nebular disturbance as a function of impact parameters and giant planet orbital architecture

Key finding: Giant planet migration excites planetesimal collisions to velocities exceeding the silicate vaporization threshold (\(v_{\rm crit,\,sil} \gtrsim 6\;\text{km/s}\) for warm forsterite), creating transient nebular shocks that process chondritic assemblages.

## 6. Synthesis: Transferable Methods for 3D Physics Visualization / Collision Modeling

The following methods from Stewart's body of work are **directly applicable** to a company developing 3D physics visualization of drones/vessels, impact/collision modeling, EOS/shock modeling, and physics-to-math pipelines:

### 6.1 SPH-Based High-Energy Collision Modeling

Stewart's group routinely uses **Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH)** with 300,000–10M particles to simulate hypervelocity impacts, tracking:
- Differential motion of core vs. mantle particles
- Vapor fraction per particle (using EOS)
- Angular momentum evolution across the full event

**Transferable:** SPH is the same engine used in modern fluid-structure interaction (FSI) for drone/vessel impact events. The methodology — particle-based, no fixed mesh, handles fragmentation naturally — transfers directly. The key lesson from Stewart's work: **the EOS matters enormously**; using the wrong thermodynamic model (e.g., Tillotson vs. M-ANEOS) can change collision outcomes by factors of 2–10.

### 6.2 Rigid-Body / Thermodynamic Equilibrium Structure Solver (HERCULES)

HERCULES solves for **equilibrium structure of a rotating, partially vaporized body** as overlapping constant-density spheroids. It computes moment of inertia \(I(L, M, S, Z)\) and derives whether the body exceeds the CoRoL.

**Transferable:** The underlying mathematical framework — iterative equilibrium structure solution with rotating self-gravitating fluid — is applicable to modeling **the structural response of large vessels or drones** under rotation + thermal/pressure loads (e.g., spin-stabilized munitions, rotating pressure vessels, centrifuge-loaded structures). The CoRoL framework is a clean analog to the structural limit concept in engineering (yield criterion as a surface in parameter space).

### 6.3 Entropy-Based Phase-Change Criteria (Vaporization Thresholds)

Stewart's work establishes that impact outcome (liquid vs. vapor vs. supercritical) is best classified by **post-shock specific entropy** \(S_H\), not pressure alone. The three-threshold framework:
- \(S < S_{\rm IV}\): no vaporization
- \(S_{\rm IV} < S < S_{\rm CV}\): partial vaporization (vapor fraction \(f_{\rm vap} \propto (S - S_{\rm IV})/(S_{\rm CV} - S_{\rm IV})\))
- \(S > S_{\rm CV}\): complete vaporization

**Transferable:** This **entropy-based regime classification** is identical in structure to the methodology needed for:
- Modeling explosive fragmentation / spallation thresholds in structural materials
- Determining when a hypervelocity impact (drone strike, terminal ballistics) crosses from cratering to perforation to catastrophic failure
- Computing energetic material states in warheads (entropy tracks irreversibility through any detonation/shock cycle)

### 6.4 Analytic Collision Scaling Laws (LS12)

The **Leinhardt–Stewart scaling laws** provide closed-form expressions for collision outcome as a function of \(\{Q_R, Q^*_{RD}, \gamma, b\}\). These are already implemented in N-body planet formation codes, and the framework:
- Maps out the full 3D parameter space of impact outcomes
- Uses dimensional analysis and \(\pi\)-scaling to extrapolate from calibrated simulations
- Provides regime-boundary curves directly usable in engineering collision classifiers

**Transferable:** The same scaling-law methodology can classify **collision outcomes between rigid bodies of arbitrary mass ratio** (e.g., drone–drone, drone–vessel, projectile–armor), providing fast first-order predictions without running full SPH. The \(Q^*_{RD}\) framework maps directly to material toughness / specific disruption energy in structural engineering.

### 6.5 Equation-of-State Pipeline: From Experiment to Hydrocode

Stewart's workflow is a gold-standard physics-to-math pipeline:
1. **Shock wave experiment** (gas gun, laser, Z-machine) → measures \((U_s, u_p, T_H)\)
2. **Hugoniot fit** → linear/nonlinear \(U_s(u_p)\) relation
3. **Entropy integration** along shock path → \(S_H(P)\)
4. **Phase boundary calibration** → sets vaporization thresholds \(S_{\rm IV}, S_{\rm CV}\)
5. **Free energy parameterization** → M-ANEOS Helmholtz potential \(F(\rho, T)\)
6. **Hydrocode embedding** → \(P(\rho, E)\) and \(T(\rho, E)\) lookup tables used in SPH/Eulerian codes

**Transferable:** This pipeline is a direct template for any application requiring **materials characterization under extreme conditions** (e.g., ballistic gelatin, composite armor, fuel tanks under hypervelocity impact, underwater pressure vessels). The mathematical structure — Hugoniot as a 1D locus in phase space, isentrope as the release path, free energy as the unifying thermodynamic potential — is material-agnostic.

### 6.6 Angular Momentum Budget Tracking

In every giant-impact simulation, Stewart's group tracks the angular momentum of every SPH particle and the total system:
\[
L_{\rm sys} = \sum_i m_i (\mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{v}_i) = \text{const}
\]
This is used to diagnose whether synestia formation occurs, how much goes into disk vs. planet, and whether resonance conditions will be met.

**Transferable:** Rigorous angular momentum tracking (with conserved budget checks at every timestep) is essential for **long-duration 3D rigid/deformable body simulations** of rotating drones, spinning projectiles, or gyroscopically stabilized vessels. Stewart's methodology — tracking \(L\) as the primary invariant and deriving spin period \(T\) from \(I(L, M, S)\) at each stage — is more robust than naive rotation-rate tracking when bodies are deforming.
