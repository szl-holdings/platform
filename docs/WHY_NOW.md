## The Moment

Three tectonic shifts have created a window for a governed decision operating system that did not exist two years ago.

### 1. The AI Liability Gap (2024–2026)

Every enterprise is deploying AI agents that make or influence consequential decisions. Most have no governance infrastructure. The gap between AI capability and AI accountability is widening every quarter.

- **EU AI Act** (effective August 2025): Requires high-risk AI systems to maintain traceability, human oversight, and documented decision lineage.
- **SEC AI Disclosure Rules** (proposed 2025): Public companies must disclose how AI influences material business decisions.
- **NIST AI RMF 1.0** (adopted 2023, expanding 2025): Federal agencies and their vendors must demonstrate AI risk management across the lifecycle.
- **State-level AI legislation**: 15+ US states have introduced AI governance bills since 2024.

Organizations that cannot prove their AI decisions are governed will face regulatory exposure, procurement rejection, and liability risk.

### 2. The Cross-Domain Intelligence Demand

Modern enterprises face cascading risks that no single-domain tool handles:
- A shipping delay affects construction timelines
- A security breach triggers regulatory disclosure obligations
- A market shift requires coordinated portfolio rebalancing

The value of intelligence compounds when signals cross domain boundaries. But cross-domain intelligence requires shared governance infrastructure — shared proof chains, shared approval gates, shared event correlation.

### 3. The Enterprise Trust Deficit

Enterprise buyers are rejecting AI tools that cannot answer three questions:
1. **What did the AI recommend and why?** (Explainability)
2. **Who approved the action?** (Accountability)
3. **Can I replay the decision?** (Auditability)

Current tools — dashboards, copilots, BI platforms — cannot answer these questions because they were not designed around the decision lifecycle.
