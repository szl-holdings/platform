# Citations

Every Horizon primitive corresponds to a published result in physics. We
list the primary references here, in chronological order, for anyone who
wants to verify the math on their own. None of this is appeal to authority;
each primitive's acceptance test is what matters operationally. The
citations exist so readers can see we did not invent the formulas.

---

## Black hole entropy & the holographic principle

- Bekenstein, J. D. (1973). *Black holes and entropy.* Phys. Rev. D 7, 2333.
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2333

- Hawking, S. W. (1975). *Particle creation by black holes.* Commun. Math.
  Phys. 43, 199. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02345020

- 't Hooft, G. (1993). *Dimensional reduction in quantum gravity.*
  arXiv:gr-qc/9310026.

- Susskind, L. (1995). *The world as a hologram.* J. Math. Phys. 36, 6377.
  https://doi.org/10.1063/1.531249

## Information theory (the bridge to computation)

- Shannon, C. E. (1948). *A mathematical theory of communication.* Bell
  Syst. Tech. J. 27, 379. https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x

- Nielsen, M. A., & Chuang, I. L. (2010). *Quantum Computation and
  Quantum Information* (10th anniversary ed.). Cambridge University Press.
  See §11.3 on quantum mutual information and §12.4 on classical-shadow
  estimators of entanglement entropy.

- Meilă, M. (2003). *Comparing clusterings by the variation of
  information.* Lecture Notes in CS 2777, 173.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-45167-9_14
  (Establishes that VI is a true metric; we use it as the entanglement
  graph distance.)
