## 1 · Khipu (knotted-cord record) — central motif

The khipu is the literal data structure of our receipt DAG (KHIPU organ, SF-06), not a
metaphor (BRAND_BIBLE §3).

- **British Museum — quipu, Am1907,0319.286** (Inca, Peru, 1430–1530). Cotton khipu with
  knotted pendant cords; one primary cord with pendant and branching subsidiary cords.
  "The word *khipu* comes from the Quechua word for knot." Curator's comments confirm the
  decimal accounting system.
  https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Am1907-0319-286
- **Smithsonian — Quipu (snapshot collection).** "The word quipu comes from the Quechua
  word for knot." Colored, spun and plied llama-hair strings; color, knot placement, and
  cord type are all part of the numerical reading. ~600 surviving khipu worldwide.
  https://www.si.edu/collections/snapshot/quipu
- **Smithsonian NMAI — Inka khipu (Infinity of Nations).** One horizontal primary string
  from which numerous pendant strings hang; length, knots, twine, and color encode
  quantitative information.
  https://americanindian.si.edu/exhibitions/infinityofnations/andes/143866.html
- **Wikipedia — Quipu** (cites Larco Museum exemplar). Knots grouped into clusters tied at
  registers along the cord; clusters represent digits in a base-10 positional system; ones
  at the bottom, then a space, then tens, etc.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quipu

### Knot typology (mathematical basis for the animation)
- **Marcia Ascher & Robert Ascher, *Mathematics of the Incas: Code of the Quipu*** — three
  knot types: simple one-turn knots (s), long knots with one+ additional turns (L), and
  figure-eight knots (E). Base-10 decimal positional system; zero = absence of a knot.
  Summarized by El País (Science), 2022-12-20:
  https://english.elpais.com/science-tech/2022-12-20/knots-representing-numbers-the-mathematics-of-the-incas.html

## 2 · Amaru (Andean serpent) — wrapping the knot

Already canon in our memory cortex flagship `amaru` (BRAND_BIBLE §5; Wiktionary `amaru`).

- **The Met — Serpent ornament, Inca (?), 1450–1532 CE**, Peru, cotton + camelid hair.
  Three-dimensional textile serpent; a fiber core shaped as a snake then wrapped in
  structural cords — directly motivates a cord-wrapped serpent rendered from the same
  cord vocabulary as the khipu. Object 1994.35.113.
  https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316938
- **The Met — Spouted Vessel with Double Headed Snake, Paracas**, 5th–4th c. BCE, Ica
  Valley, Peru. Primary evidence for the double-headed serpent in Andean iconography.
  Object 63.232.82.
  https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/308485
- **Wiktionary — `amaru`**: serpent / viper; double-headed serpent in Andean iconography.
  https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/amaru
