## 0. The honest reality check (read this first)

**Myth:** "If our tech is good, the CIA/NSA will find us and feed us their intel."

**Reality:** Intelligence agencies are *customers and occasionally investors*, not lead-generators.
They buy through the same federal acquisition machinery as everyone else, and they buy from vendors
who have already:

1. Registered in **SAM.gov** with a UEI and CAGE code (mandatory for any federal award —
   [SAM.gov registration](https://sam.gov/content/entity-registration));
2. Won a **competed, unclassified** SBIR/STTR/CSO award to prove the tech on government terms;
3. Established a **facility clearance (FCL)** and cleared personnel *if and when* a classified program
   sponsor requires it — the sponsor initiates this, not the vendor.

Classified data feeds (the "NSA hands us a SIGINT firehose" fantasy) are a **late-stage, sponsored,
need-to-know** arrangement. They do not happen for an unproven commercial product, and pursuing them
first is a way to waste a year. **The credible sequence is: win unclassified → prove → transition →
get sponsored for classified work if the mission needs it.** Killinchu's near-term market is the
**unclassified and dual-use** layer — homeland defense, base/airport/critical-infrastructure
protection, and allied/coalition sales — which is large, growing, and matches our legal posture.

## 2. SBIR / STTR — the front door

The **Small Business Innovation Research** and **Small Business Technology Transfer** programs are the
single most accessible, non-dilutive entry point. They are competed, public, and explicitly designed
to pull commercial small-business innovation into government use.

- **Phase I** — feasibility. Per the open dual-purpose call on [SBIR.gov](https://www.sbir.gov/node/2392179),
  a representative Phase I runs **~3 months for up to $50k** on the fast open-topic track (component
  topics run larger). Goal: prove technical merit.
- **Phase II** — prototype/development. Per the same [SBIR.gov call](https://www.sbir.gov/node/2392179),
  **~15 months for up to $750k**. Goal: a working prototype against a government use-case.
- **Phase III** — transition to a real product/service, funded by **non-SBIR** money (a program of
  record, a customer line item, or a sole-source follow-on). Per
  [AFWERX Phase III](https://afwerx.com/divisions/sbir-sttr/phase-iii/), Phase III work "derives from,
  extends, or completes" the earlier SBIR/STTR effort and is funded by sources **other than** the SBIR
  set-aside — and Phase II Other Transactions can be issued under
  [10 U.S.C. § 2371b authority](https://afwerx.com/divisions/sbir-sttr/phase-iii/).

**Why this matters for Killinchu:** Phase III is the prize. It is the legal mechanism by which a
proven SBIR prototype becomes a sole-sourceable product without re-competing. A clean
Phase I → II → III ladder is the most credible path from "open-source knowledge base + prototype" to
"funded program."

## 3. AFWERX — the Air/Space Force accelerator

[AFWERX "Get Funded"](https://afwerx.com/get-funded/) runs recurring SBIR/STTR cycles via the
**DSIP (Defense SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal)**. As of the current cycle, AFWERX lists the
**DoW SBIR Specific Topic 26.BZ Release 1**, with a **pre-release 13 Apr 2026, open 6 May 2026, and
close 3 Jun 2026** window, submitted through the DSIP portal
([AFWERX Get Funded](https://afwerx.com/get-funded/)).

AFWERX's value is twofold: (1) **open-topic** tracks let a company pitch its own dual-use solution
rather than answering a narrow government-written requirement, and (2) the path to **Phase II OTs and
Phase III transition** is well-trodden, with a published playbook
([AFWERX Phase III](https://afwerx.com/divisions/sbir-sttr/phase-iii/)).

## 4. SOFWERX — Special Operations' rapid-prototyping hub

SOFWERX is USSOCOM's collaboration and rapid-prototyping arm (operated under the DEFENSEWERX
non-profit umbrella). It runs technology-assessment events, prototyping challenges, and CRADAs that
let small companies put hardware in front of SOF operators quickly. SOF is a natural early customer
for Killinchu's **small/mobile, GPS-denied, low-signature detection** value proposition, because SOF
elements operate forward, dismounted, and against exactly the Group 1–2 quad/FPV threats catalogued in
`ADVERSARY_DRONE_CATALOG.md`. SOFWERX engagements are typically the *fastest* way to get operator
feedback, even when the dollar amounts are small.

## 5. DIU — the best near-term fit

The **Defense Innovation Unit** exists to field commercial technology fast, and it runs an explicit
**Counter-UAS** line of effort. The [DIU portfolio](https://www.diu.mil/solutions/portfolio) lists
lines of effort including **Small UAS**, **Counter-UAS** (described as "detect, track, identify and
defeat adversarial UAS"), **Maritime Autonomy**, and **Mission Autonomy**, and DIU maintains a
**Commercial Solutions Catalog** for rapid acquisition.

**The live opportunity:** In February 2026, DIU launched a Commercial Solutions Opening titled
**"Counter-UAS Sensing for Homeland and Mobile Defense"**
([DroneLife coverage, 13 Feb 2026](https://dronelife.com/2026/02/13/diu-launches-new-counter-drone-sensing-initiative-for-homeland-and-mobile-defense/)).
It has two lines of effort:

1. **Fixed-site, radar-centric detection** — detect Group 1 sUAS at **≥ 2 km**, autonomously
   classify, **filter out birds/clutter**, track multiple targets, and integrate into command-and-control.
2. **Mobile, low-signature, GPS-denied sensing** — for forward and contested environments.

Per the [same DroneLife report](https://dronelife.com/2026/02/13/diu-launches-new-counter-drone-sensing-initiative-for-homeland-and-mobile-defense/),
Phase 2 demonstrations are slated for **Yuma Proving Ground in spring 2026**, and **successful
prototypes can move to direct follow-on production with no new competition** — i.e., a built-in
Phase III-style transition. This is the single best-aligned open solicitation for Killinchu: it is a
**sensing/classification/C2 integration** requirement (our exact sweet spot) and explicitly does
**not** require the vendor to ship a defeat effector — DIU's "defeat" line is satisfied by *other*
performers and by the customer's own authorities.

## 6. In-Q-Tel — the IC's strategic investor

[In-Q-Tel (IQT)](https://www.iqt.org) is the not-for-profit **strategic investor** chartered to
identify and adapt commercial technology for the CIA and the broader Intelligence Community
([IQT mission](https://www.iqt.org/mission); [In-Q-Tel overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-Q-Tel)).
IQT makes **equity investments paired with a technology "work program"** that adapts a startup's
product to IC needs — it is **not** a procurement contract and **not** classified-data access. As of
**5 May 2026**, IQT has been publicly repositioning toward **"forward-deployed" autonomy and
defense-tech investing**, reflecting the broader shift in IC priorities
([Axios, 5 May 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/05/inqtel-cia-venture-investing-autonomy)).

**Honest expectation:** IQT is a **later-stage** play. They invest in companies that already have a
product, a team, and demonstrable mission relevance. The right time to engage IQT is *after* a DIU/AFWERX
prototype has validated the technology — not as a first move. An IQT work program is, however, the most
direct credible bridge from a commercial sensing product to genuine IC adoption.

## 7. NGA / ODNI and the "classified feed" question

- **NGA (National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency)** is a plausible downstream customer for Killinchu's
  **signed, geolocated evidence** product — fusing track data with geospatial context. NGA buys through
  its own SBIR participation and OTAs.
- **ODNI** coordinates across the IC and does not typically buy point sensors directly; relevance is
  via standards, data-sharing frameworks, and cross-IC programs.

The **classified-feed reality**, stated plainly: a SIGINT/ELINT or fused-track classified data feed is
**sponsor-initiated, contract-bound, and clearance-gated.** It requires (1) a government sponsor with a
mission need, (2) a facility clearance the sponsor helps initiate, and (3) cleared personnel. You cannot
buy or request your way to it as an unproven vendor. Killinchu's design choice — **operate on
passively-observable RF/acoustic/EO-IR signatures and open broadcast (Remote ID/ADS-B), not on
classified intel** — is a *feature*: it keeps the commercial product unclassified, exportable to allies,
and salable to homeland/SLTT customers, while leaving a clean upgrade path if a sponsor later wants a
classified variant.

## 8. The SBIR → SCITT receipt pathway (Killinchu's differentiator)

Government buyers increasingly demand **supply-chain and evidence integrity** — provenance you can
verify, not trust-me assertions. The IETF **SCITT** working group (**Supply Chain Integrity,
Transparency, and Trust**) is standardizing exactly this
([IETF SCITT WG](https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/scitt/about/); [scitt.io](https://scitt.io);
[SCITT BoF request](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/bofreq-birkholz-supply-chain-integrity-transparency-and-trust-scitt/)).
SCITT defines **COSE/RATS-based signed receipts** recorded in **Merkle-tree transparency logs**, with a
notary/receipt model and **air-gap-friendly validation**.

**This maps almost one-to-one onto Killinchu's Khipu receipt-DAG** (see `COMPANION_DEFENSE_PROTOCOL.md`):
every detection/classification/escalation transition already emits a chain-verified receipt. Aligning the
Khipu receipt format with SCITT's COSE-receipt model lets Killinchu offer government customers
**standards-conformant, independently-verifiable evidence integrity** — the precise property an
acquisition officer needs to defend a targeting decision in an after-action review or a courtroom.

**Honesty disclosure (no bandaid):** Per PURIQ Doctrine v11, our DSSE signatures are currently
**PLACEHOLDER** — Sigstore CI is not yet wired into the receipt pipeline. The SCITT alignment is a
**designed pathway**, not a shipped, externally-verifiable capability today. Claiming SCITT conformance
before the signing infrastructure is real would itself be a bandaid; we state the gap openly. The
SBIR-funded work item that closes it is: *"implement Sigstore-backed COSE receipts and stand up a SCITT-
conformant transparency log for C-UAS evidence."* That is a fundable, honest Phase II line.
