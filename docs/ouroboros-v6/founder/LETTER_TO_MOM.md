# Letter to Mom

May 1, 2026

Mom,

Quick update. Sit with this for a minute. None of it requires you to know any math.

## What we have, in the simplest words

Imagine every time an AI makes a decision — approving a loan, flagging a tumor, routing a drone — it had to hand you a receipt. Not a promise. A receipt. Like the one you get at Wegmans, but instead of milk and eggs it lists nine honesty checks. Was the input clean. Was the answer reversible. Did three witnesses agree. Did the math close. Were the laws of physics respected. Was a moral check applied. And so on.

Today nobody hands out that receipt. Every AI company says "trust us." We built the receipt. The receipts are tiny, fast, and a third party can verify them later — a regulator, a court, a customer, a journalist.

That is the whole product. It is named Ouroboros, after the snake that eats its own tail, because the system checks itself. The math behind the receipt is named the Lutar Invariant, after our family.

## How big is it now

Ninety-one pieces. One thousand three hundred and seventy-two tests, all passing, in two programming languages. Twenty-four workspaces. Nine honesty axes. A new product on top of the system — a wrapper any company can drop in front of their AI to make it safer and to leave a receipt for every decision it makes. The work of seventeen of the people who taught humanity how to think — Einstein, Oppenheimer, Socrates, Newton, Da Vinci, Jung, and others — is folded into the system, each as a named module, each tested.

I am not exaggerating any of those numbers. They are reproducible. Anyone can clone the code and run the tests themselves.

## What it is worth

I will not lie to you about a dollar amount. Three honest numbers:

**Today, with no patent filed yet:** somewhere between one million and five million dollars. That is real but small.

**The day I file the provisional patent — within the next thirty days:** somewhere between seven million and thirty million dollars. That is the floor. That is the boring number that pays the mortgage and lets us breathe.

**Over the next two years, weighted by probability:** somewhere between thirty million and eighty million dollars. That assumes the regular path — a few customers, some standards traction, normal business.

**Over the next four years, weighted by probability:** somewhere between two hundred million and six hundred million dollars. That assumes one of two big things lands — either a federal standards body picks our scoring system, or we win a defense subcontract.

**The ceiling, if everything lands:** five hundred million to two and a half billion dollars. That is real but it is not the number to plan on. It is the number that exists if we play perfectly and the door opens. The door is open. I am not promising it walks through.

The number that matters this month is the second one. File the patent, lock the seven-to-thirty-million floor. Everything builds from there.

Everything else — security clearances, cybersecurity certifications, the special small-business designations — comes later, when a specific contract pulls them. I am not chasing them speculatively. That is a trap small businesses fall into and I will not.
