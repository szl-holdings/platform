## 1. Executive Summary

Three certifications carry 80 percent of the revenue unlock available to SZL Holdings in the next 24 months. SAM.gov registration is free and executes in two hours; it is the mandatory federal gateway for every contract, grant, and cloud marketplace listing that follows. SOC 2 Type 2 costs $20,000–$40,000 all-in and unlocks Fortune 500 procurement, federal civilian RFPs, and every serious enterprise evaluation process. cloud marketplace listing costs nothing beyond developer time and immediately opens private-offer contracting with any AWS customer, including federal agencies buying through GovCloud. Everything else is conditional.

StateRAMP is not on the execution path today. The cost of a StateRAMP LI-SaaS or 20x Low authorization runs $150,000–$300,000 and demands a committed agency sponsor before the investment is rational. The correct trigger is a named federal prospect asking for an ATO, not a founder anticipating one. CMMC is equally premature; no DoD contract, no CMMC obligation. ISO 27001 adds value only when EU revenue becomes a material pipeline segment, not before. The 8(a) set-aside designation is worth evaluating at Month 6 once the two-year business age clock has advanced, but it is not a precondition for anything else.

The Mercy McInnis meeting on May 6, 2026 is a procurement counseling session with a Empire APEX Empire APEX advisor whose function is to help companies navigate SAM.gov, identify set-aside eligibility, and position for state and federal contracting. That meeting has a specific, actionable ask described in Section 6.

## 5. Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| SAM.gov TIN validation failure causes registration delay | Medium — EIN/IRS name mismatch is the most common cause of failed SAM submissions | High — blocks all federal contracting eligibility | Verify EIN/TIN against IRS records before submitting; confirm exact legal name matches IRS records character-for-character; budget 10 business days for re-submission if validation fails |
| SOC 2 Type 2 observation window reveals unresolved control gaps, delaying report | Medium — a one-person organization has limited redundancy in access review, incident response, and change management controls | High — delays Fortune 500 and federal sales cycle; compliance platform subscription cost continues | Begin evidence collection on Day 1 of platform onboarding; run quarterly internal access reviews and simulated incident response exercises during observation window; engage auditor for interim control check at Month 9 |
| cloud marketplace integration rejected due to metering API non-conformance | Low — technical requirement is well-documented but requires correct implementation of ResolveCustomer and BatchMeterUsage calls | Medium — delays market distribution by 2–6 weeks | Follow AWS SaaS integration checklist from [Labra guide](https://labra.io/how-to-list-your-saas-on-aws-marketplace-step-by-step-guide-for-2025/); test entitlement callbacks in sandbox before submitting for review; use automation platforms (Clazar, Suger) if internal dev time is constrained |
| StateRAMP agency sponsor fails to materialize by Month 18 | Medium-High — federal agency procurement cycles are long; securing a sponsor without an existing relationship is difficult for a pre-revenue company | High — $150,000–$300,000 StateRAMP investment is unrecoverable without a contract | Gate C is a hard gate: do not authorize StateRAMP capital without a named sponsor or confirmed 20x pilot acceptance; the Mercy McInnis counseling session is the first step toward building the relationships that produce sponsors |
| EU AI Act classification as high-risk triggers $10,000–$50,000 compliance obligation before EU revenue exists | Low to Medium — Ouroboros governance runtime is most likely limited-risk, but use-case-specific deployments (employment, credit, benefit eligibility) could trigger high-risk classification | Medium — diverts capital from SAM and SOC 2 priorities | Complete EU AI Act risk classification exercise at Month 6; document the classification rationale with legal review; do not deploy to EU customers in high-risk use cases until Article 9–17 compliance is confirmed |

## 6. The Mercy Ask

Mercy McInnis is a procurement counselor at Empire APEX Empire APEX, a New York State-funded procurement technical assistance center. The specific ask for the May 6, 2026 meeting is this: walk through the SAM.gov registration in real time and confirm that SZL Holdings's NAICS codes (541512 primary, 541511 and 541715 secondary) and PSC codes (DA10 primary, DJ10 secondary) are correctly positioned for AI governance SaaS contracting. Ask Mercy to identify any active or upcoming New York State and federal AI governance solicitations that SZL Holdings should be monitoring, and request a specific opinion on whether Stephen meets the social and economic disadvantage criteria required for 8(a) Business Development Program eligibility. Ask whether Empire APEX maintains a list of federal contracting officers or program managers who are actively building AI governance acquisition pipelines, and whether Empire APEX can make an introduction. Finally, confirm whether the SBIR/STTR programs at NSF or DoD have active topics aligned with verifiable AI trust scoring that SZL Holdings should apply to. The [SBA contracting guide](https://www.sba.gov/federal-contracting/contracting-guide/basic-requirements) and [SAM.gov checklist](https://sam.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/entity-checklist.pdf) should be in hand before the meeting.
