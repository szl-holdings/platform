v9 of the Ouroboros thesis (Lutar 2026) bound every formula in the Lutar Invariant family v1..v7 plus the unified master invariant L_Omega to six implementation artefacts: an exported function, a typed knowledge-graph node, an HTTP route, a guardrails contract test, a section in the canonical thesis chain, and a row in the operator surface of the runtime A11oy. The binding was asserted in prose. In v10 we turn the assertion into a single computable scalar.

For each layer L_k we define a per-layer audit observable A_k = L_k * prod_j 1[j_k], where j ranges over the six artefact dimensions {CODE, CODEX, API, TEST, THESIS, SURFACE} and 1[j_k] is a presence indicator obtained by direct repository inspection. The Audit Closure Operator is Lambda_10 = sum_k A_k. We prove the closure theorem: the ratio rho = Lambda_10 / sum_k L_k equals 1 iff every artefact is present for every layer. The operator is strictly inert in the all-operational limit (Lambda_10 = sum_k L_k by direct expansion of the indicator product) and quantifies operational drift by exactly the missing fraction otherwise.

We implement Lambda_10 as a deterministic exported function, a Zod-validated HTTP route, and an eight-test contract suite covering closure, drift detection, conservation, and theorem-string identity, all green against the live shipping repository (HEAD ce3229ebe, 2026-05-05). The live audit returns rho = 1 and an empty missingArtifacts array, confirming the v9 chain as fully operational. We argue that meta-invariants of this form -- machine-verifiable contracts over a versioned formula family -- are an underused instrument in the publication-ready maintenance of long-running mathematical software. Lambda_10 introduces no new physical quantity; v9 supplies the L_k values. Code, tests, and runtime are public.
