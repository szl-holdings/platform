The modern SOC has solved detection.

It has not solved governance.

Alert correlation is better. Ingestion pipelines are broader. Response playbooks are faster. And yet: when a compliance officer asks what authorized a containment action during an incident, and what evidence supported it, most organizations reconstruct the answer from ticket exports and Slack messages.

That is the accountability gap. Not a failure of analyst diligence. A structural failure of the tooling.

Governed SOC operations requires four capabilities that most current platforms do not provide:

→ Authorization records at the action level — not just who reviewed the alert, but who confirmed the specific action, with what role, supported by which evidence chain, evaluated against which policy

→ Evidence chains attached to recommendations — every AI recommendation carries its correlated events, technique mapping, confidence score, and ranked alternatives

→ Policy enforcement at the action gate — containment actions, identity suspensions, and external escalations pass through a Covenant Policy check before execution. Not a flag. A gate.

→ Outcome linkage for learning — the causal path from action to outcome, feeding back into AI calibration after the incident closes

Aegis is the SZL Holdings domain pack built on this architectural foundation: a SOC command surface where governance is structural, not documentary.

The compliance environment is making this distinction matter more. Organizations can demonstrate detection capabilities. The next requirement is demonstrating governed decision-making at the SOC layer.
