The standard critique of multi-vertical platforms: you can't build deep domain expertise across six industries simultaneously.

This critique is correct for traditional software. It is not correct for a platform built on shared governance infrastructure.

Here's the structural argument:

A company building governed AI specifically for maritime operations builds a deep Proof Chain for voyage decisions, Covenant Policies reflecting maritime regulatory requirements, and an Outcome Graph accumulating voyage decision history.

Then a client asks: can you help with counterparty credit risk assessment?

They have three options: decline, build a separate module (rebuild all governance infrastructure from scratch), or partner (incomplete integration).

None of these produces what the client needs: a single governed decision environment where maritime and credit intelligence are in the same Outcome Graph, evaluated by the same Proof Chain, surfaced through the same interface.

The domain pack architecture solves this by separating two concerns:

**Domain knowledge** — data sources, decision types, terminology, regulatory requirements for a vertical. Domain-specific. High complexity.

**Governance infrastructure** — the structural layer enforcing accountability, provenance, and learning on every consequential decision. Domain-agnostic. Built once.

A competitor building governed AI for maritime and then for real estate builds two Proof Chains, two Outcome Graphs, two policy enforcement engines. Governance infrastructure rebuilt from scratch each time.

The cross-domain intelligence advantage is the hardest to replicate: patterns that are invisible in any single domain — a beneficial owner maintaining exposure across maritime, real estate, and legal structures simultaneously — emerge only from a shared cross-domain Outcome Graph.

The moat is the compound of domain pack expansion, Outcome Graph depth, and governance investment. It grows with time. Single-domain tools cannot replicate it structurally.
