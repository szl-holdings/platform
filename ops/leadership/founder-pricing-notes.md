# Founder Pricing Notes — SZL Holdings

**Phase:** F · **Audience:** Founder only (internal) · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

**This document is internal. Pricing is not published. See "Why Pricing Is Not Public" below.**

---

## Pricing Philosophy

SZL Holdings prices for the value of governance, not the cost of software. Customers buy the platform because consequential decisions cost six figures or more when wrong. The platform's price reflects that risk reduction, not the dev cost of a SaaS product.

**Core principle:** Premium pricing, transparently arrived at, never apologized for. Buyers who flinch at the price either don't have the pain we solve, or aren't the buyer profile.

---

## Pricing Anchors (Indicative, Internal)

These are anchor prices — starting points for the founder's pricing conversation. Final prices are deal-specific and reflect scope, scale, and commitment.

### Tier 1 — Pilot (Design Partner)

| Form | Anchor |
|------|--------|
| First 5 design partners (anchor accounts) | $0 — free in exchange for case study + reference |
| Design partner cohort 6-15 | $5K-$15K nominal pilot fee (signals seriousness) |
| Pilot beyond cohort 15 | $25K-$50K (pilot becomes a paid evaluation, no longer "design partner") |

**Rationale:** First 5 design partners are anchor case studies. The platform's commercial proof comes from them. Free is correct for that cohort.

### Tier 2 — Production (Single Domain Pack)

| Pack | Anchor (annual) | Per-seat / usage component |
|------|----------------|----------------------------|
| Lyte | $50K-$150K base | $200-$500 / operator / month |
| Aegis | $75K-$200K base | $500-$1K / operator / month |
| Vessels | $50K-$150K base | $1K-$3K / vessel / year |
| Terra | $40K-$120K base | $5K-$15K / portfolio market / year |
| Carlota Jo | Custom (advisory engagement, not SaaS) | N/A |

**Rationale:**
- Base fee covers platform infrastructure, support, audit trail, and ongoing development
- Variable component scales with the customer's value extraction
- These ranges should land in market — too high signals greed; too low signals weakness

### Tier 3 — Enterprise (Multi-Pack, Custom)

| Form | Anchor |
|------|--------|
| Multi-pack starting price | $300K-$500K annual |
| Strategic / institutional deal | $750K-$2M+ annual |
| Multi-year commitment discount | 10-15% per year of multi-year |

**Rationale:** Enterprise deals are negotiated. The anchor is the floor; the ceiling is what the buyer's value model justifies.

## Pricing Levers (When to Adjust)

### Increase price (vs. anchor) when:
- Buyer is in a high-margin industry (financial services, energy trading, defense)
- Buyer has high decision-stakes ($1M+ per decision)
- Buyer is in a crowded competitive landscape and wins on speed (we're the speed)
- Buyer's compliance burden is heavy and we materially reduce it

### Decrease price (vs. anchor) when:
- Buyer is anchor reference for a new domain pack (give discount in exchange for reference rights)
- Buyer is in early commercial cohort (cohort 6-15 in Tier 2)
- Buyer is taking on integration risk (early adopter of a new connector)
- Multi-year commitment with annual upfront payment

### Never decrease price by:
- Cutting the base fee while keeping scope (signals "I really wanted this deal")
- Throwing in services for free (devalues services)
- Bundling add-ons silently (destroys add-on revenue)

### Always decrease scope, not price, when buyer pushes back
- "We can drop these 2 use cases from the pilot scope" → keeps price integrity
- "We can do half the operator seats" → keeps price integrity
- "We can defer this integration to phase 2" → keeps price integrity

## Why Pricing Is Not Public

**Public pricing on the website would:**
- Anchor every conversation on a number, not on value
- Eliminate the discovery → joint diagnosis sequence (buyers pre-judge)
- Force premature standardization across very different buyer profiles
- Telegraph commercial structure to competitors
- Lock in pricing that should evolve with proof

**The pricing page reads:**

> *"Pricing reflects deployment scope, integration complexity, and commercial commitment. We're happy to discuss specifics in a scoped conversation."*

This is honest, not coy. It signals: "Talk to us. We will give you a real number for your real situation."

---

## Pricing Conversation Script

When a buyer asks "what's it cost?" early in a discovery conversation:

> *"I'd rather give you a real number than a generic one. Once I understand the use cases and the scope, I can be specific. Range-wise: pilot is in the low five figures or no charge for design partners; production for a single domain pack is in the mid five-to-six figures annually depending on operator count and integrations; enterprise multi-pack starts in the six figures and goes up from there. Let me ask you a few questions and I can be more specific."*

This:
- Refuses to be evasive
- Provides honest ranges
- Re-anchors on scope, not price
- Moves the conversation forward

---

## Pricing Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Offering a discount before the buyer asks.** Telegraphs you're insecure about price.
2. **Quoting the floor.** Quote the anchor; let scope justify movement.
3. **Bundling without itemization.** Buyers don't know what they're getting; they devalue everything.
4. **Pricing the pilot like a product.** The pilot is to prove the loop. It is not where the revenue lives.
5. **Inconsistent pricing across deals.** If two similar buyers got 3x different prices, the lower-priced one will hear about it from the network and lose faith.

---

## Pricing Audit Cadence

- **Per-deal:** Founder logs every quoted price (range and final) in a private tracker
- **Quarterly:** Review the tracker. Look for: drift below anchors, inconsistency across similar buyers, win/loss correlation with price
- **Annually:** Adjust anchors if observed pricing has drifted >25% in either direction

---

## What to Tell Investors About Pricing

Investors want to know:
- ARR per customer (use anchor + buyer mix)
- Pricing power (anchor-to-final ratio; if final is consistently below anchor, pricing power is weak)
- Pricing trajectory (anchors should rise as proof accumulates)
- Customer acquisition cost vs. annual contract value

**Investor-facing summary:**
- Tier 1 (pilot): low five figures or free for design partners
- Tier 2 (production): $75K-$300K annual depending on pack and scale
- Tier 3 (enterprise): $300K-$2M+ annual
- Average production deal trajectory: $150K → $300K → $500K as accounts expand domain packs

---

## What to Tell the Press About Pricing

Don't. Pricing is for buyer conversations, not press conversations. Press asks → "We discuss pricing in scoped conversations with prospects."

---

*Pricing is leverage. Used poorly, it destroys margin and signals weakness. Used well, it filters for the buyers who actually have the pain. Premium, transparent in conversation, never public, never apologized for.*
