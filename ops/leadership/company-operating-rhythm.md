# Company Operating Rhythm — SZL Holdings

**Phase:** H · **Audience:** Founder, internal · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

---

## Purpose

A founder-stage company without an operating rhythm runs on heroics, miscommunication, and missed cycles. With a deliberate operating rhythm, the same founder produces 3-5x more proof per quarter with less stress.

This document defines the daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, and annual rhythms of SZL Holdings during the design partner and early commercial phase (2026 — first ~24 months of commercial motion).

---

## The Cadence Stack

```
Daily      → focus and pipeline
Weekly     → partner reviews and sales motion
Monthly    → metrics review and strategic alignment
Quarterly  → strategic review and roadmap
Annually   → vision, fundraise, market position
```

Each layer has a fixed time, fixed agenda, and fixed output.

---

## Daily Rhythm

### Morning block (1 hour, 8:00-9:00)
- Review yesterday's notes and today's calendar
- Identify the 1-3 outcomes that must happen today (the "today's wins")
- Process inbox to zero (responses or punted with explicit follow-up)

### Maker block (3 hours, 9:00-12:00)
- Deep work: strategic writing, product spec, deal proposal, demo prep
- No meetings, no Slack, no email
- One block, one outcome

### Pipeline block (1 hour, 12:00-13:00 OR 17:00-18:00)
- Pipeline movement: send 3-5 outreach messages, follow up on 3-5 active conversations, log progress in tracker
- Daily, no exceptions, even when the pipeline feels full

### Meeting block (3-5 hours, 13:00-18:00)
- Discoveries, demos, partner reviews, joint diagnoses
- Batch where possible (one demo day per week, one partner review day per week)

### Evening close (15 minutes, end of day)
- Write the day's notes (what happened, what's pending, what's next)
- Pre-stage tomorrow's "today's wins"

### Daily output
- Inbox at zero
- Pipeline tracker updated
- Day's notes written
- Tomorrow's wins pre-staged

## Weekly Rhythm

### Monday — Strategy & Prep
- 1-hour weekly review (alone): last week's outcomes, this week's priorities
- 1-hour pipeline review: every active conversation, next action, next gate
- 2-hour maker block: strategic writing or product work
- Afternoon: discoveries / demos as scheduled

### Tuesday-Thursday — Execution
- Weekly partner reviews (1 hour per active pilot)
- Discoveries and demos
- Joint diagnoses
- Maker block in the morning
- Pipeline block daily

### Friday — Synthesis & Reset
- 2-hour weekly synthesis: write the weekly note (see "Weekly Founder Note" below)
- 1-hour planning block for next week
- 1-hour content / thought leadership work (LinkedIn, Substack, etc.)
- Optional: end the day at 5:00 — recover

### Weekly outputs
- All weekly partner reviews completed and notes written
- Pipeline tracker fully current
- Weekly founder note written (see below)
- Next week's calendar set

### The Weekly Founder Note (every Friday)
A 1-page internal note covering:
- Pilots: status of every active pilot, this week's progress
- Pipeline: top 5 active conversations, next gate for each
- Product: critical bugs, shipped features, gaps surfaced
- Founder: what the founder learned this week, what's blocked, what they need
- Next week: 3 priorities

This note is the single most important written artifact of the company in the design partner phase. It is the input to the monthly review and the quarterly review. Skip it and the company loses its memory.

## Monthly Rhythm

### First Monday of the month — Monthly Review
- 3-hour structured review of the month
- Attendees: founder + advisor (when one is engaged) + key contractor leads (when relevant)

### Monthly Review agenda (3 hours)

```
0:00 - 0:30  Pilots: status of every pilot, conversion progress
0:30 - 1:00  Pipeline: top 10, conversion rate, sourcing health
1:00 - 1:30  Product: shipped, in-progress, gaps surfaced this month
1:30 - 2:00  Operations: financial, ops, legal, infrastructure
2:00 - 2:30  Strategic: what we learned this month, what's changing
2:30 - 3:00  Decisions: what we'll do differently next month
```

### Monthly outputs
- Monthly metrics dashboard (see `founder-control-room-v2.md`)
- Pipeline conversion rates updated
- Product gaps prioritized for next month
- Strategic decisions captured in writing
- Next month's 3-5 priorities set

## Quarterly Rhythm

### Quarterly Strategic Review (full day, end of each quarter)
- Half day: review the quarter (what worked, what didn't, what we learned)
- Half day: set the next quarter's strategy

### Quarterly Review agenda (8 hours)

```
Morning (4 hours):
- Detailed pilot retrospective (every pilot, every metric, every lesson)
- Pipeline retrospective (sourcing, qualification, conversion)
- Product retrospective (shipped, gaps, roadmap accuracy)
- Financial retrospective (revenue, runway, burn)
- Strategic retrospective (positioning, market signals, competitor moves)

Afternoon (4 hours):
- Next quarter's primary objective (single sentence)
- Next quarter's 3-5 strategic bets
- Next quarter's pilot targets (count, profile, conversion goal)
- Next quarter's product roadmap
- Next quarter's commercial targets (revenue, retention, expansion)
- Next quarter's manual actions (founder commitments)
```

### Quarterly outputs
- Quarterly retrospective document (5-10 pages)
- Next quarter's strategy document (3-5 pages)
- Updated category positioning if material change (see `one-sentence-category-statement.md`)
- Updated investor narrative if material change

## Annual Rhythm

### Annual Strategic Offsite (3 days, end of fiscal year)
- Day 1: Year retrospective — what we built, what we learned, what changed
- Day 2: Vision update — where the category is going, where SZL is going
- Day 3: Annual plan — themes, hires, fundraise, market positioning

### Annual outputs
- Annual retrospective (10-20 pages)
- Updated 3-year vision
- Annual operating plan (themes, headcount, fundraise targets)
- Investor narrative refresh
- Public-facing year-in-review (if appropriate)

---

## Cadence Health Indicators

The operating rhythm is healthy if:
- ✅ Founder writes the weekly note 4 of 4 weeks per month
- ✅ Monthly review happens within 7 days of month-end
- ✅ Quarterly review happens within 14 days of quarter-end
- ✅ Pilots have weekly reviews on the agreed cadence
- ✅ Pipeline tracker is updated within 24 hours of any state change

The operating rhythm is failing if:
- ❌ Weekly note is skipped (founder is too busy = the company has no memory)
- ❌ Monthly review is deferred (the company is reactive, not deliberate)
- ❌ Pilot weekly reviews are skipped (the most leveraged hour of the week)
- ❌ Pipeline goes more than a week without sourcing activity (60 days later, pipeline is empty)

---

## What the Rhythm Protects Against

| Risk | How rhythm mitigates |
|------|---------------------|
| Founder reactivity | The maker block protects deep work |
| Missed pilot signals | Weekly partner reviews catch problems early |
| Pipeline drought | Daily pipeline block keeps inflow steady |
| Strategic drift | Monthly and quarterly reviews force alignment |
| Lost lessons | Weekly note captures the founder's learning |
| Burnout | Friday synthesis + weekend recovery is structural |

---

## Rhythm Adjustments by Stage

### Design partner phase (now — first 5-15 pilots)
- All rhythms above
- Founder is heavily in the meeting block
- Maker block protected aggressively (it's the only deep work happening)

### Early commercial phase (15-50 pilots, post-PMF)
- Daily and weekly cadence intensifies (more pilots, more pipeline)
- Monthly cadence adds: customer success, hires, ops formalization
- Add: 1-on-1s with first 2-3 hires (weekly)

### Scale phase (50+ customers, post-growth capital)
- Founder transitions from doing to enabling
- Daily and weekly cadence delegates to ops/CS leads
- Founder maintains: monthly review, quarterly review, annual offsite, top 10 customer relationships

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **No rhythm at all.** Founder runs on inbox and panic. Productivity collapses by month 3.
- **Rhythm without enforcement.** Calendar exists but slips immediately. Same as no rhythm.
- **Rhythm without output.** Meetings happen but no notes, no decisions, no continuity.
- **Cargo-culted rhythm.** Copying someone else's cadence without adapting to your stage.
- **Rhythm that prevents work.** If meetings consume the maker block, the rhythm is wrong.

---

*The operating rhythm is the founder's most important system. It is the difference between "we shipped a lot" and "we built a company." Run the rhythm. Adjust it as the company grows. Never let it lapse.*
