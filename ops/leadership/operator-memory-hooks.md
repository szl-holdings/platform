# Operator Memory Hooks

**Owner:** Stephen Lutar · **Audience:** Founder, demo runners, GTM writers

Buyers do not remember features. They remember **phrases that describe a category**. Every line below should be repeatable, defensible, and tied to something they just saw on screen during the demo killer path.

## The seven hooks

### 1. "Governed Decision Infrastructure"
**Use when:** Opening any conversation, intro slide, executive-command landing.
**Why it lands:** It is a category, not a product. It positions us above agentic-AI tooling.
**On screen:** Operating Loop Rail across the top of the executive command screen.

### 2. "One screen that matters."
**Use when:** Showing the executive command / executive summary view.
**Why it lands:** Executives have been burned by dashboards. This promises the opposite.
**On screen:** The KPI strip + top signals + value-at-risk on a single screen, no scroll.

### 3. "Every recommendation has a receipt."
**Use when:** Opening a decision receipt for the first time.
**Why it lands:** Buyers have never seen this from agentic-AI vendors. It is the single most differentiating phrase in the demo.
**On screen:** Decision receipt with scoring factors, evidence, and alternatives considered.

### 4. "A signal is not a notification — it carries provenance."
**Use when:** Opening a signal detail panel.
**Why it lands:** Reframes the entire alerting category. Notifications are noise; signals are accountable.
**On screen:** Signal detail with source, source type, owner, value-at-risk, tags.

### 5. "Enforced at the policy layer; bypass requires explicit, attributed override record."
**Use when:** Sitting on the approval chain in the approvals center.
**Why it lands:** This is the truth-pass rule in a single sentence. It is the language compliance buyers want to hear.
**On screen:** Approval chain with tier, approver identity, SLA, and the (rare) override path.

### 6. "Decisions become work, not memos."
**Use when:** Showing the action queue.
**Why it lands:** Differentiates execution from analysis. Most decision tools stop at the recommendation; we close the loop into work.
**On screen:** Action queue tiered by urgency, with state transitions and dependencies.

### 7. "Auditable by default — the receipt is the system of record."
**Use when:** Closing on the trust & audit log.
**Why it lands:** Every other vendor offers an audit log as a feature. We position it as the *underlying contract*.
**On screen:** Immutable audit log with outcome pills, actor types, and resource IDs.

## How to use these hooks

- **Pick three per demo.** Never use all seven in one conversation. Pick the three that match the buyer (compliance, ops, executive).
- **Always tie each hook to something they can see right now.** A hook with no on-screen referent becomes marketing. A hook tied to a UI element becomes belief.
- **Repeat the same phrase three times in a deal cycle.** Once on the demo, once in the recap, once in the proposal. Repetition is what turns a phrase into a category claim.

## Phrases to retire

- "AI-powered." Replaced by "governed."
- "Insights." Replaced by "recommendations with receipts."
- "Workflow automation." Replaced by "policy-gated execution."
- "Observability." Replaced by "decision observability" — and only when the audit log is on screen.
- "Copilot." We are not a copilot; we are an accountability layer.
