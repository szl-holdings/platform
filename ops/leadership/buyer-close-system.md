# Buyer Close System — SZL Holdings

**Phase:** E · **Audience:** Founder · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

---

## Purpose

A repeatable system for moving a qualified buyer conversation from "interested" to "signed pilot" or "signed production contract." This is not a sales playbook copied from SaaS; it is the close motion specific to selling Governed Decision Infrastructure to a category buyer who has never bought this category before.

---

## The Six-Step Close Motion

```
Discovery → Demo → Joint diagnosis → Scoped pilot proposal → Mutual agreement → Signed
```

Each step has a question it answers. If the question is not answered, do not advance.

---

### Step 1 — Discovery

**Question to answer:** *Does this buyer have a real, expensive, recurring decision pain that the platform addresses?*

**What founder does:**
- 30-45 minute conversation, no demo, no deck
- Ask about the most consequential decisions the buyer makes weekly
- Ask how the buyer would know, today, if a decision was wrong
- Ask what their cost of a bad decision is — $, time, reputational
- Ask who in the org makes the decision, who reviews it, who audits it
- Listen for: signal fragmentation, unclear ownership, audit pain, AI without governance

**What founder does NOT do:**
- Pitch the platform
- Show slides
- Quote a price

**Advance gate:** Founder can write 3 sentences describing the buyer's specific decision pain, with a $ or time number attached.

---

### Step 2 — Demo

**Question to answer:** *Does the platform's loop demonstrably address the pain we surfaced in discovery?*

**What founder does:**
- 45-60 minute demo of the canonical loop (signal → context → recommendation → simulation → policy → execution → proof → outcome → learning)
- Anchor the demo in the buyer's specific pain — replace generic signals with signals from their domain
- Show the audit trail. Pause. Let it land.
- Show one operator override. Let them see the human-in-the-loop reality.

**What founder does NOT do:**
- Demo features that don't map to the buyer's pain
- Promise capabilities the platform doesn't have
- Show roadmap as if it were current state

**Advance gate:** Buyer asks at least one question that signals they are imagining their own operation in the platform ("how would this work if our signal source was X?", "what happens when our compliance team reviews this?").

---

### Step 3 — Joint Diagnosis

**Question to answer:** *What specifically would a 90-day pilot prove for this buyer?*

**What founder does:**
- 60-minute working session with buyer's ops lead and ideally one operator
- Co-author a one-page diagnosis: "Here is the decision pain, here is the baseline metric we'd watch, here is what success looks like"
- Identify the 1-3 use cases the pilot would cover (NOT all use cases — narrow scope wins)
- Identify the 5-10 baseline metrics to capture at Day 0
- Identify the operator(s) who will be in the loop during pilot

**Advance gate:** Buyer signs off on the diagnosis document. If they hesitate to sign off, the pilot is not ready.

---

### Step 4 — Scoped Pilot Proposal

**Question to answer:** *Does the pilot scope, terms, and commitment work for both sides?*

**What founder does:**
- 1-2 page pilot proposal: scope (use cases), duration (90 days), success metrics, commercial terms (free during pilot or pilot fee — see `pilot-vs-production-commercial-model.md`), what each side commits to
- Send proposal within 48 hours of joint diagnosis
- Schedule a 30-minute review call to walk through it

**Advance gate:** Buyer's economic decision-maker (CFO, COO, or principal) is on the call when the proposal is reviewed. If they're not, the pilot is not real yet.

---

### Step 5 — Mutual Agreement

**Question to answer:** *Are both sides fully aligned on what is being committed to?*

**What founder does:**
- Walk through the proposal line by line
- Confirm: success metrics, operator availability, weekly partner review attendance, baseline data sharing, what happens at Day 90 (renewal? production contract? exit?)
- Capture any negotiated changes in writing

**Advance gate:** Both sides verbally agree on every term. Founder summarizes in an email within 24 hours.

---

### Step 6 — Signed

**Question to answer:** *Is there a signed agreement on file?*

**What founder does:**
- Send the design partner agreement (if pilot) or commercial contract (if production)
- Use a simple, short agreement (5-10 pages, not 50). Bias toward clarity.
- Follow up at Day 3, Day 7, Day 14 if not signed
- If not signed by Day 21, surface the blocker explicitly

**Closed gate:** Signed agreement on file. Pilot kickoff scheduled within 2 weeks.

## Closing Specifics

### What to Say When the Buyer Stalls

| Stall | What it usually means | Response |
|-------|----------------------|----------|
| "We need to think about it" | Step 3 was incomplete; pain is not crisp | Go back to discovery; surface the specific pain |
| "Let me check with my team" | The economic decision-maker isn't bought in | Ask to include them in the next call |
| "Can you reduce the scope?" | They want a smaller commitment, which is fine | Reduce to 1 use case; keep success metrics tight |
| "What about [unrelated feature]?" | They are looking for reasons not to sign | Acknowledge, defer, return to the agreed scope |
| "We're not ready" | They don't trust the platform yet | Offer reference call; do not push |
| Silence after the proposal | The proposal didn't land | Call (don't email); surface the silence directly |

### What to Say When You're About to Lose the Deal

> *"It sounds like the pilot scope we discussed isn't matching what you actually need. Help me understand what would make this a no-brainer for you. I'd rather understand the gap and walk away than push you into a pilot that won't deliver."*

This sentence saves more deals than any closing trick. Buyers respect honesty over pressure.

### What NEVER to Say

- "This is the last day for this pricing" — false urgency destroys trust
- "Our other customers are seeing 10x" — vague metrics destroy credibility
- "We can do anything you need" — over-committing destroys the relationship
- "Let me have my team [do work to convince you]" — buyer didn't ask for it; you look desperate

## Pacing the Close Motion

| Buyer Type | Realistic Cycle Time |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Founder-led startup (50-200 employees) | 2-6 weeks discovery to signed pilot |
| Mid-market with internal champion | 6-12 weeks |
| Enterprise (security, fleet, regulated) | 12-26 weeks |
| Government / institutional | 26+ weeks (use enterprise close pack — see `enterprise-close-pack.md`) |

If a deal is moving faster than this, scrutinize: is the buyer skipping due diligence?  
If a deal is moving slower, scrutinize: which step is unanswered?

---

## What "Signed" Looks Like at Each Tier

| Tier | Signed Artifact | Commercial Form |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Design partner (pilot) | 5-page design partner agreement | Free or nominal pilot fee, 90-day term |
| Production starter | Standard subscription contract | Monthly or annual, single domain pack |
| Production enterprise | Enterprise contract + DPA + security exhibit | Annual, multi-domain pack, custom SLA |
| Strategic / institutional | Master agreement + statements of work | Annual or multi-year, enterprise close pack |

## Anti-Patterns

- **Demoing before discovery.** You will demo features that don't map to pain. Buyer disengages.
- **Skipping joint diagnosis.** Pilot starts without agreed success criteria. Day 90 is a coin flip on whether buyer renews.
- **Negotiating without economic decision-maker.** You'll re-negotiate later when they appear.
- **Long contracts.** Enterprise prospects accept 50 pages; design partners do not. Match the contract to the relationship.
- **Discounting in the close.** Discount in the scope reduction, not the price. Price discounts signal weakness.

---

*The close motion is repeatable. Run it the same way every time. Variance kills predictability; predictability is the foundation of a commercial engine.*
