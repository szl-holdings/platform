# Message Architecture — SZL Holdings

**Phase:** K · **Audience:** Every buyer-facing surface, every founder-led conversation · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

---

## The Six Elements

Every founder-led conversation, sales call, demo, deck, and public surface draws from these six elements in order. They are designed to be modular — a 30-second elevator pitch is element 2 + element 6; a 60-minute investor meeting walks all six.

| # | Element | What it answers | Length |
|---|---------|-----------------|--------|
| 1 | Company statement | Who we are at the company level | 1 sentence |
| 2 | Category statement | What category we are creating / leading | 1 sentence |
| 3 | Buyer problem | What hurts the buyer enough to act | 3-5 sentences |
| 4 | Why existing tools fail | Why the buyer cannot solve this with what they already have | 5 sentences (one per competitor class) |
| 5 | Why SZL is different | The six pillars, stated as differentiators | 6 sentences |
| 6 | What the buyer gets | Concrete outcome the buyer takes home | 3 sentences |

## Element 1 — Company Statement

> **SZL Holdings is a privately-held technology company that builds governed decision infrastructure for organizations where the cost of a poorly-attributed decision is measured in millions of dollars, regulatory exposure, or human consequence.**

**Variants:**
- Press: "SZL Holdings, founded in 2025 by Stephen Lutar, is a privately-held technology company building governed decision infrastructure for high-stakes operations."
- Investor: "SZL Holdings is a pre-seed technology company that has built a governed decision platform with six fully-implemented domain packs, currently in design partner phase."
- Recruiting: "SZL Holdings is a founder-led technology company building the governance layer for enterprise AI — the first technical hire joins a platform with six live domain packs and a unified mobile command surface already shipped."

## Element 3 — Buyer Problem

> **Enterprise operations have an accountability gap. Dashboards show what happened. Alerts show what is wrong. AI tools add recommendation volume without governance. Decisions run in parallel — in Slack threads, email chains, hallway conversations — with no attribution, no simulation, no audit trail, and no closure mechanism. When a decision goes wrong, no one can reconstruct the chain. When a regulator asks "who approved this and on what evidence?", the answer is a screenshot of a chat window.**

**Buyer-specific variants:**

| Buyer | Problem framing |
|-------|----------------|
| CISO | "Your SOC sees alerts. Your team triages them. Decisions get made in Slack. When the auditor asks why a finding was closed, the evidence is a chat screenshot. That is the gap." |
| Fleet executive | "Your fleet manager sees AIS data. The compliance team checks sanctions lists. The commercial team models voyage P&L. None of these decisions live in the same audit trail. When a charter goes wrong, the post-mortem is a slide deck." |
| NYC broker / investor | "You see distress signals from public data. You decide which deals to pursue based on intuition and a spreadsheet. There is no record of why a deal was passed on — and the next time the same signal appears, you cannot tell whether you have learned anything." |
| Managing partner / counsel | "Matter triage, conflict checks, retainer decisions — all judgment calls. No structured recommendation, no attribution to the partner who approved the engagement, no audit if the matter blows up two years later." |
| CEO / COO | "You have dashboards in five places. Decisions get made in meetings. Approval queues live in email. When something goes wrong, the post-mortem is a forensic exercise across four tools. There is no decision system." |

---

## Element 4 — Why Existing Tools Fail

> **The tools the buyer already has do not close the gap, and here is why each category stops short:**
>
> **Generic AI copilots** (ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Glean) generate recommendation volume without schema, without provenance, without policy gates, and without outcome tracking. The recommendation evaporates into chat history.
>
> **Observability tools** (Datadog, Splunk, Grafana) are best-in-class at signal collection but stop at "what happened." They do not say what to do, who is accountable, or what the simulated consequence of acting is.
>
> **Workflow tools** (ServiceNow, Zapier, n8n) execute pre-defined sequences. They do not decide what should run, simulate the consequence, or enforce approval gates at the engine layer.
>
> **Trust and compliance vendors** (Vanta, Drata, OneTrust) sell the binder for the auditor. The product is not used during the workday — it is used before an audit.
>
> **Vertical roll-ups and specialty tools** give domain depth in one vertical and zero leverage across verticals. Each new domain is a separate stack, separate governance, separate audit model.

---

## Element 5 — Why SZL Is Different

> **SZL Holdings is structurally different on six pillars:**
>
> **1. Governed execution.** Every consequential action passes through the same nine-step loop — Signal, Context, Recommendation, Simulation, Policy, Execution, Proof, Outcome, Learning — enforced at the library layer, not the UI.
>
> **2. Evidence-backed actions.** Every AI recommendation carries model identity, source citations, confidence scores, classification, and export safety status. Schema-validated. Not prose.
>
> **3. Attributable automation.** Every action carries an actor, a role, a timestamp, and an evidence chain. Manual, semi-autonomous, and autonomous modes all generate the same audit record.
>
> **4. Operator-first design.** Every command surface is built for the person who is going to push the button — not for analysts, administrators, or executives reading a report.
>
> **5. Trust built into workflow.** Trust surfaces are operator pages used during the workday, not compliance pages used before an audit. Proof Chain runs on every recommendation.
>
> **6. Domain pack extensibility.** Six domain packs — Aegis, Vessels, Terra, Counsel, Carlota Jo, IMPERIUM — ride one shared governance substrate. New domain packs ship with governance on day one.

## Element 6 — What the Buyer Gets

> **The buyer gets a command surface their operators use every day, a governance layer that is structural rather than optional, and an audit trail that satisfies regulators without a separate compliance project. Every recommendation is sourced. Every action is attributed. Every outcome is recorded. The platform compounds: as the buyer adds domain packs, governance does not need to be re-bought, re-built, or re-certified — it is the substrate.**

**Buyer-specific variants:**

| Buyer | What they get |
|-------|---------------|
| CISO | "Aegis Defense workspace, with every SOC decision attributed and audit-ready. Add Aegis Command for managed-services. Add Aegis Intelligence for AI governance. Same governance substrate, three workspaces." |
| Fleet executive | "Vessels Command surface with AIS, sanctions screening, voyage simulation, and dark vessel detection — every commercial decision routed through governance, every approval attributed, every outcome closed." |

| CEO / COO | "Lyte command surface with PRISM framework, command inbox, action queue, approvals center — one operating loop across the org, one audit trail, one closed-loop learning system." |

## How to Sequence the Six Elements

### 30-second elevator
Element 2 (category) + Element 6 (what the buyer gets, summary).

### 5-minute pitch
Element 2 → Element 3 → Element 5 (named pillars only) → Element 6.

### 30-minute discovery call
Element 3 (buyer-specific framing) → Element 4 (only the two competitor classes the buyer mentions) → Element 5 (focus on 2-3 pillars relevant to buyer) → Element 6 (buyer-specific).

### 60-minute investor meeting
Element 1 → Element 2 → Element 3 → Element 4 → Element 5 → Element 6 → demo → Q&A.

### Demo opening (90 seconds)
Element 2 stated, Element 5 named (six pillars in one breath), then transition to demo.

### Written collateral (deck, one-pager)
Slide / page 1: Element 2.
Slide / page 2: Element 3.
Slide / page 3: Element 4.
Slide / page 4-5: Element 5 (one slide per pillar group).
Slide / page 6: Element 6.

## Forbidden Drift

The following phrases must not appear in any of the six elements. They are commodity AI / generic SaaS language that weakens the moat:

- "AI-powered"
- "Intelligent automation"
- "Smart [anything]"
- "Next-generation"
- "Transformative"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "Revolutionary"
- "Best-in-class" (use specific evidence instead)
- "Seamless integration"
- "Single pane of glass" (use "command surface" instead)
- "Enterprise-grade" (assume it; do not claim it)
- "Leveraging AI"
- "Democratizing [anything]"

(Full audit in `no-commodity-ai-language.md`.)

## How to Test the Architecture With a Real Conversation

A buyer should be able to retell the message after one hearing. After a 30-minute call, ask them: *"How would you describe what we do to a colleague?"*

**Pass:** The colleague hears category (element 2), problem (element 3), and at least three pillars (element 5).

**Partial pass:** The colleague hears category and problem but cannot name the pillars.

**Fail:** The colleague says "they do AI for operations" or "it's like Datadog with AI."

If the test fails, the failure is in the founder's delivery, not in the message. Re-read this document and the `founder-message-pack.md`.
