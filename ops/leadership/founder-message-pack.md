# Founder Message Pack — Stephen Lutar

**Phase:** K · **Audience:** Stephen, internal use · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

---

## How to Use This Pack

Five conversation contexts. Each one has an opener, a discovery sequence, the response to the most common objection, and a close. Memorize the openers. Internalize the discovery sequences. Have the closes ready.

These are not scripts to read. They are the language patterns the message has been compressed into.

## Context 1 — Sales Calls (Operator / Buyer)

### Opener (first 60 seconds)

> *"Thanks for the time. Before we get into your situation — let me tell you in two sentences what we do, so the rest of the call lands properly.*
>
> *SZL Holdings is the governed execution layer for enterprise intelligence. Every consequential decision in your operation — security finding, sanctions hit, distress signal, matter triage — passes through the same loop: signal, context, recommendation, simulation, policy gate, execution, proof, outcome. Six domain packs ride that substrate today.*
>
> *Now — tell me about how decisions get made on your team today, and where the accountability falls apart."*

### Discovery sequence (questions, in order)

1. *"When something consequential needs to be decided in your team — not a routine ticket, but something that has financial or regulatory weight — walk me through how that decision actually happens today."*
2. *"Where in that flow is the AI recommendation coming from? Is it sourced? Is it attributed?"*
3. *"If a regulator or auditor asked tomorrow 'who approved this and on what evidence,' what would you hand them?"*
4. *"How do you know whether the decision was the right one, 90 days later?"*
5. *"If you had a single command surface where every consequential decision had a signal source, a simulated outcome, a policy gate, an attributed approval, and a recorded result — would that solve the problem you just described, or is the problem somewhere else?"*

### Most common objection

**"We already have [Datadog / ServiceNow / Vanta / Microsoft Copilot]."**

> *"Keep it. We are not those things. Datadog tells you the cluster is hot — we tell you who acted on it. ServiceNow runs the workflow — we decide whether the workflow should run, simulate the consequence, and gate the approval. Vanta sells the binder — we sell the operating system the binder falls out of. Microsoft Copilot makes recommendations — we turn recommendations into governed decisions with evidence and outcome.*
>
> *The question is not whether to replace your tools. The question is whether you have a layer above them that closes the decision loop."*

### Close

> *"Here is what I propose. Pick one decision in your operation that has cost you in the last six months — a delayed approval, a sanctions miss, a deal you regretted. Let us run it through the platform on a 90-day design partner pilot. You will see the loop end-to-end on your own data. If at the end of 90 days you do not see structural value, we walk away friends. If you do, we talk commercial terms. Fair?"*

## Context 2 — Investor Calls (Pre-Seed / Seed / growth capital)

### Opener (first 90 seconds)

> *"Three things up front, then I'd like to hear what you'd want to validate.*
>
> *One — SZL Holdings is the governed execution layer for enterprise intelligence. New category. Not a copilot, not a dashboard, not a workflow tool. The structural layer between signal detection and action execution where governance, attribution, simulation, proof, and outcomes are the platform — not features bolted on after the sale.*
>
> *Two — we have shipped six fully-implemented domain packs on one shared governance substrate: security, maritime, real estate, legal, advisory, cloud. Plus a unified mobile command surface (CORTEX) that reflects the same loop on the device. This is approximately 18-24 months of a 3-4 person engineering team's output, delivered by a single founder with AI assistance over the last 12 months.*
>
> *Three — we are pre-revenue by design. The current phase is design partner program. Three design partners before billing activation. Stripe is built and ready; activation is configuration, not engineering.*
>
> *What would you want to validate?"*

### Discovery sequence

1. *"What is your thesis on enterprise AI governance — passing fad, structural shift, or somewhere in between?"*
2. *"Where do you see the boundary between AI-as-feature and AI-as-infrastructure?"*
3. *"How do you weight architectural depth versus revenue traction at the pre-seed / seed stage?"*
4. *"What is the bar for 'category-creating' versus 'feature-of-an-incumbent' in your portfolio?"*

### Most common objection

**"You have no revenue. How do I know there is a market?"**

> *"Three answers. First — the market is the same one buying Vanta, Datadog, ServiceNow, and Microsoft Copilot. Those vendors have validated the spend. The question is not whether the market exists; it is whether they will pay for the layer above their tools.*
>
> *Second — the architecture is validated. Six domain packs on one substrate is not a thesis; it is shipped code. A vertical SaaS competitor would have to build six stacks. A horizontal copilot would have to retrofit governance onto an existing model. Neither has done it.*
>
> *Third — the design partner motion is in flight. The funding ask is to convert the architectural moat into commercial proof. We are not asking you to underwrite the engineering. We are asking you to underwrite the GTM motion against an architecture that already exists."*

### Close

> *"What I would suggest is — review the repo. Six domain packs, 685 database tables, 51 shared packages, governance libraries, mobile command surface. Read the trust center. Read the moat document at `ops/leadership/moat-definition.md`. Then we have a follow-up where you tell me what would make this fundable for your fund. I will be straight with you about whether we are a fit."*

## Context 3 — Demos

### Opener (first 30 seconds, before any screen)

> *"Quick frame before I show you anything. Every screen you are about to see is one step in the same nine-step loop: signal, context, recommendation, simulation, policy, execution, proof, outcome, learning. Same loop in security, in maritime, in real estate, in legal. The platform is the loop. The domain packs are the signal sources and action vocabularies on top.*
>
> *I am going to walk you through one signal end-to-end. Pay attention to the audit trail at the end — that is the proof everything you saw actually happened the way I described."*

### Demo discovery sequence (during the demo)

- *"Notice that this recommendation has a model identity, a source citation, and a confidence score. That is not a UI feature. The schema is validated at the API boundary. A copilot will not show you these because the data does not exist."*
- *"This approval gate is enforced at the policy engine library, not the UI. If your developer tried to bypass it in a script, the bypass would be recorded as an attributed override. The gate is structural."*
- *"This simulation panel is showing you the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentile outcomes from a Monte Carlo run on the recommendation. Your operator sees consequence before they push the button."*
- *"At the end you will see the proof chain — every step, attributed, immutable. This is what the auditor sees. It is also what the operator uses to learn from the decision."*

### Most common demo objection

**"This looks like a lot. How quickly does an operator get productive?"**

> *"The operator does not need to learn the loop. They need to learn one screen — the command surface for their domain pack. The loop runs underneath. In a typical pilot, an operator is independently using the platform within two days. The governance, simulation, and audit trail are happening for them, not by them."*

### Close

> *"I'd like to do this same walk-through against one of your real signals next time. We can stand up a sandboxed environment with one of your data sources connected. You will see your own data in the loop. That is the moment buyers usually decide whether this is structural or theater."*

## Context 4 — Partner Conversations (Channel / Marketplace / Integration)

### Opener (first 60 seconds)

> *"I want to be clear about how we are positioned, because that affects whether the partnership makes sense.*
>
> *SZL Holdings is the governed execution layer for enterprise intelligence. We sit above tools like [partner's product] — your product is a great input to our decision engine, our trust surface, or our action engine. We are not competing with you for the same buyer; we are extending what your buyer can do with your data.*
>
> *What I want to explore is — where in your customer base are buyers asking for governance, attribution, or AI-grade audit, and how do we build a motion that puts the SZL layer in front of those conversations?"*

### Discovery sequence

1. *"Where are your customers feeling pain that your product is not designed to solve?"*
2. *"Are your customers asking for AI governance / decision attribution / outcome tracking?"*
3. *"What does your channel motion look like — referral, co-sell, OEM, marketplace?"*
4. *"What would a successful partnership look like for you in 90 days?"*

### Most common objection

**"We are building this ourselves."**

> *"Understood. Two questions before you decide. First — are you building one domain or six? Because the leverage of a shared governance substrate only shows up when you have multiple domains riding it. Second — are you building governance as a feature in one product, or as a substrate that any product surface can ride? Because those are very different engineering problems.*
>
> *If you are building governance as a feature in your product, we are not competitive. If you are building it as a substrate, we should compare notes — because that is what we have done."*

### Close

> *"Let me send you the moat document and the platform primitives doc. Read them. If after reading them you still want to build it, that is the right answer for you. If you want to talk about a referral, co-sell, or marketplace path, we should set up the next conversation."*

## Context 5 — Recruiting (First 5 Hires)

### Opener (first 90 seconds)

> *"I want to tell you what you would be joining — because the scope of what we are building either lights you up or it does not.*
>
> *SZL Holdings is the governed execution layer for enterprise intelligence. We have shipped six domain packs on one governance substrate, plus a unified mobile command surface. The architecture is operational. What we are not yet doing — at scale — is selling it.*
>
> *The first five hires are not engineers building features. They are operators turning architectural depth into commercial reality. That is a rare seat. If you join, you are not picking up tickets — you are owning a domain end-to-end. Sales, customer success, design partner ops, founding engineer for a specific surface — every one of those seats reports to the founder and gets to define the playbook.*
>
> *What is the work you want to be doing in 18 months?"*

### Discovery sequence

1. *"What is the company you joined that you are most proud of, and why?"*
2. *"What is the part of your current job that you would not miss?"*
3. *"What is your bar for autonomy versus structure at this stage of your career?"*
4. *"What would make a startup not work for you — culturally, technically, or commercially?"*

### Most common objection

**"You have no revenue. Why would I leave a paid job for this?"**

> *"Right answer for most candidates. The wrong answer for the candidate I am looking for. The candidate I am looking for has seen enough product to know that architecture this deep, on a category this clear, is rare. They are willing to bet six months of equity on whether the GTM motion lands. If you are not that candidate, I should not waste your time. If you are — let us talk about what your seat looks like."*

### Close

> *"Read the moat document. Read the README. Read the trust center. If after reading them you want to talk about what the first 90 days look like for you specifically, we set up a working session. We do not do interview loops. We do working sessions."*

## Cross-Context Rules

### Rules for every conversation
1. **State the category in the first 60 seconds.** Always element 2 from the message architecture.
2. **Never use commodity AI language.** Refer to `no-commodity-ai-language.md`.
3. **Always reference the architecture, not features.** Six pillars, one loop, six domain packs.
4. **Always close with a concrete next step.** Pilot, repo review, working session, follow-up.
5. **Never promise what is not yet built.** Honest about pre-revenue, honest about SOC 2 timing, honest about live data versus seeded data.

### Rules for moments when the conversation drifts
- If they are listing competitor names: snap back with the competitor positioning sentence from `competitive-positioning.md`.
- If they are using commodity AI language: do not match it. Restate the specific pillar that addresses what they think they are asking about.
- If they are asking for a feature you do not have: say *"that is on the roadmap; let me show you what we have built today, and you can tell me where the priority is for you."*
- If they are pushing on revenue / customers: pivot to architecture validation + design partner motion + funding-converts-to-GTM logic.

### Rules for tone
- Confident but specific. No hype.
- Founder voice. Not sales voice.
- Short sentences. Active verbs.
- Acknowledge what is not built. Do not over-claim.
- Use the canonical category sentence in every conversation. Same words. Repetition is the moat.
