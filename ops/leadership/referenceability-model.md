# Referenceability Model — SZL Holdings

**Phase:** B · **Audience:** Founder, customer success · **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-16

---

## Purpose

A reference account is the single most leveraged asset in early-stage commercial motion. One willing operator at a real buyer, who can speak to the platform's outcome in their own words, closes more deals than a year of marketing.

This document defines the path from "design partner" to "production reference" — the stages, gates, and obligations on both sides.

---

## The Five Referenceability Stages

```
Pilot signed → Pilot active → Pilot completed → Reference (private) → Reference (public)
```

Most pilots stop at Stage 3. The discipline is moving them, deliberately and explicitly, to Stages 4 and 5.

---

### Stage 1 — Pilot Signed

**State:** Buyer has signed the design partner agreement. No outcomes yet.

**What we say externally:** Nothing about the buyer publicly. Internal only.

**What buyer commits to:** 90-day pilot, weekly partner review, baseline data sharing, honest feedback.

**What SZL commits to:** Direct founder access, white-glove onboarding, no charge during pilot.

---

### Stage 2 — Pilot Active

**State:** Pilot is in motion. Decisions running through the loop. Outcomes accruing.

**What we say externally:** May reference industry/region (e.g., "we're piloting with a NYC commercial brokerage"); no buyer name, no logo.

**What buyer commits to:** Continued engagement, weekly review attendance, candid feedback.

**What SZL commits to:** Founder responsiveness, product fixes within agreed SLAs, no surprise upcharges.

---

### Stage 3 — Pilot Completed

**State:** 90-day pilot is over. Outcomes measured. Case study draft in buyer review.

**What we say externally:** Anonymized case study (Tier 1) with buyer permission. No name yet.

**What buyer commits to:** Sign off on anonymized case study or decline externalization.

**What SZL commits to:** Honor whatever tier the buyer grants; no pressure for higher tier.

**Decision gate:** Does the buyer continue (Stage 4 path) or exit (no further commitment)?

---

### Stage 4 — Reference (Private)

**State:** Buyer is in production use (paid). Has agreed to take reference calls from qualified prospects.

**What we say externally:** Logo on customer wall (with permission). Named case study with metrics. May quote operator (with permission).

**What buyer commits to:** Up to 4 reference calls per quarter. Quarterly check-in with founder.

**What SZL commits to:** Cap reference calls at 4 / quarter. Pre-brief the prospect so the call respects buyer's time. Refresh talking points annually.

**Talking points memo includes:**
- What the buyer will speak to (specific outcomes, specific use cases)
- What the buyer will NOT speak to (specific commercial terms, internal politics)
- The operator's preferred call format (30 min phone, async email, recorded video)

---

### Stage 5 — Reference (Public)

**State:** Buyer is willing to co-market with SZL. Joint podcast, panel, white paper, conference appearance, or major editorial feature.

**What we say externally:** Full named case study, attributed quotes, logo, joint content. Buyer is part of the SZL story.

**What buyer commits to:** Co-creating content. Speaking publicly about outcomes. Being a named anchor reference.

**What SZL commits to:** Buyer typically gets thought leadership credit. Joint content created with buyer's marketing team in the loop. Buyer reviews any content before publication.

**This is the highest tier.** Treat it as the rare asset it is.

## Reference Account Portfolio Strategy

Aim for, by Day 365 of commercial motion:

| Stage | Target count | Why |
|-------|--------------|-----|
| Stage 4 (private reference) | 5-7 | Cover every domain pack with at least one reference per pack |
| Stage 5 (public reference) | 1-2 | One anchor case study per fundraise / category-defining moment |

A portfolio of 5+ private references and 1-2 public references is sufficient to support a growth capital motion and an enterprise sales pipeline of ~50 active conversations.

---

## What Earns a Reference Account

Buyers move to Stage 4 because:
1. **The pilot worked.** Quantified outcome > 20% improvement on a baseline metric.
2. **The relationship works.** Founder responsiveness, no commercial bait-and-switch.
3. **The platform stayed honest.** No over-claiming, no surprise gaps.
4. **The buyer's reputation is enhanced.** They look smart for picking SZL early.

Buyers do NOT move to Stage 4 if:
1. The pilot delivered the platform but not the outcome.
2. Commercial terms changed unexpectedly post-pilot.
3. The product had a critical gap that surfaced too late.
4. The founder over-promised and the buyer feels burned.

The honesty discipline is the path to referenceability. Drift kills it.

## Reference Account Hygiene

| Activity | Cadence | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| Quarterly check-in with reference operator | Every 90 days | Catch problems early |
| Talking points refresh | Annually | Keep claims aligned with current product state |
| Cap on reference calls | 4 / quarter / reference | Don't burn the relationship |
| Send curated prospect briefs before each call | Before every call | Respect the buyer's time |
| Thank-you note after each call | After every call | Maintain the relationship |
| Annual reference dinner / event | Annually | Build the portfolio's network |

## When a Reference Account Goes Cold

Signals:
- Operator stops responding to check-in emails
- Sentiment in casual conversation shifts negative
- Buyer organization changes (new CISO, new fleet manager, etc.)
- Product change degrades the buyer's experience

Response:
1. Pause use of the buyer as a reference immediately
2. Get on a call with the operator to understand
3. If repairable: address the root cause, restore the relationship, restart references
4. If not repairable: drop the reference, replace with another buyer at the same tier

A cold reference used as warm is a deal-killer when the prospect calls and gets a flat response.

---

## Reference Account Asks (How to Set the Expectation Up Front)

When signing the design partner agreement, set the expectation that:

1. The pilot is mutually exploratory; either side may decline to externalize
2. If outcomes are good, SZL will request anonymized case study (default)
3. If the buyer is open, named case study and reference call participation can follow
4. The buyer always has veto over what is said publicly about them
5. There is no commercial penalty for declining to be a reference

Setting this expectation up front avoids awkward asks later and signals SZL's respect for the buyer's autonomy.

---

## What a Reference Conversation Sounds Like

When a prospect asks "can I talk to a reference?", the founder's response:

> *"Yes. I can introduce you to one of our pilot partners who is in [their industry / region]. Before I do, let me brief them on who you are and what your situation looks like, so they can speak to the parts of their experience that are most useful to you. The call will be 30 minutes, scheduled by my team. They will speak honestly about what worked and what didn't — that's the only kind of reference I am willing to provide."*

This frames the reference as honest, prepared, and respectful — which is what closes the deal, not a gushing testimonial.
