# Design Partner Offer

**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Defines who qualifies, what partners receive, what they commit to, and how success is measured.

---

## What Is a Design Partner

A design partner is an organization that co-validates the SZL Holdings platform in a structured pilot — in exchange for early access, preferred pricing, and direct founder input into the roadmap.

Design partners are not paying customers in the traditional sense. They are commercial co-creators. The relationship is explicitly mutual: the partner gets a platform that is shaped by their real operational needs; SZL Holdings gets domain validation, baseline-to-delta proof, and referenceable outcomes.

This is honest about stage. Design partners know they are early. That is the point.

---

## Who Qualifies

### Hard Criteria (all required)

- [ ] Operates in one of the covered domains: security/defense, maritime, real estate, legal/professional services, or multi-domain operations
- [ ] Has a specific operational problem that maps to a domain pack capability
- [ ] Has an identified executive sponsor with decision authority
- [ ] Has a technical POC who can support integration (if applicable)
- [ ] Can commit a 60–90 day pilot window with defined success metrics
- [ ] Is genuinely willing to engage — not just collecting demos or vendor research

### Soft Criteria (strong preference)

- Organization size: 50–5,000 employees (mid-market sweet spot)
- Some appetite to discuss commercial terms post-pilot
- Willingness to consider referenceability after a successful pilot
- Domain operations that generate enough signal volume to test the platform meaningfully

### Disqualifying Factors

- Organizations that want unlimited access with no engagement commitment
- Organizations in active budget freeze with no path to commercial conversion
- Domains SZL Holdings does not currently serve (do not accept partners to build speculative domain packs)
- Organizations that require SOC 2 certification before any access (not yet certified — do not misrepresent timeline)

## What Partners Receive

### Platform Access
- Full access to the relevant domain pack and all underlying primitives (Lyte, Alloy, Proof Chain, etc.)
- CORTEX mobile access for key users (iOS or Android)
- Designated sandbox workspace isolated from other partners
- Role-based access for up to 10 users (larger teams by agreement)

### Founder Engagement
- Weekly async check-ins (signal log review + blockers)
- Bi-weekly 30-minute sync calls
- 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day formal review sessions
- Direct roadmap input: documented and tracked, not just acknowledged

### Commercial Preferment
- Design partner pricing locked for 12 months post-pilot
- Design partner pricing is at a minimum 30% below projected standard commercial pricing
- Priority consideration for expanded access and new domain packs
- First right of refusal on any exclusive or limited-availability features

### Knowledge Assets
- Written baseline document before pilot (capturing current state)
- Delta report at 60 and 90 days (measuring change)
- Lessons document synthesizing what worked and what to improve
- Case study draft if partner agrees to be referenceable

## What Partners Commit To

### Time
- Executive sponsor: 1–2 hours per month
- Domain practitioner(s): 2–4 hours per week of active use
- Technical POC: 4–8 hours upfront for integration setup, then as-needed
- Bi-weekly sync calls (30 min) with founder
- 90-day review session (60–90 minutes)

### Engagement Standards
- Maintain signal and evaluation logs weekly (with support from founder)
- Report blockers within 24 hours — do not let issues fester
- Provide honest feedback at each checkpoint — sugarcoated feedback does not help either party
- Keep pilot terms confidential if requested (platform capabilities, commercial terms)

### Referenceability (Good Faith Discussion)
- Partners agree to have an honest referenceability conversation at 90 days
- They are not required to be named references — but they agree to discuss it
- If they decline referenceability, they agree to provide written feedback on why

## Pilot Success Metrics

Success metrics are defined before the pilot starts. Generic metrics are insufficient — each pilot needs specific, measurable targets agreed in advance.

**Framework for setting metrics:**

1. Pick 2–3 metrics that matter to the partner (not metrics that make SZL look good)
2. Capture baseline values before the pilot starts
3. Agree on what "success" looks like at 90 days for each metric
4. Define attribution confidence expectations: what external factors might affect the metric?

**Example metrics by domain:**

| Domain | Example Metric |
|---|---|
| Aegis (security) | Time from threat detection to approved response action |
| Vessels (maritime) | Percentage of voyage P&L exceptions reviewed vs. missed |
| Terra (real estate) | Deal evaluation cycle time (signal to approval) |
| PRISM Counsel (legal) | Matter review time per attorney per week |
| General | Decision audit completeness (% of consequential decisions with full attribution) |

**What to avoid:**
- Vanity metrics (number of logins, pages viewed)
- Metrics the partner cannot measure today (no baseline = no delta)
- Metrics that require capabilities the platform does not yet have

## Design Partner Tiers

### Standard Design Partner
- Single domain pack
- Up to 10 users
- 60-day pilot standard, 90-day option

### Strategic Design Partner
- Two or more domain packs
- Up to 25 users
- 90-day pilot with optional 30-day extension
- Named co-creator credit in press and case study materials
- Quarterly roadmap session with founder

### Enterprise Design Partner
- Full platform access
- Unlimited users within defined org scope
- 90-day pilot with structured transition to production
- Custom commercial terms and SLA
- Dedicated founder time for first 30 days of pilot

## Offer Document Delivery

When presenting the design partner offer to a qualified prospect:

1. Share this document (or a cleaned version without internal notes)
2. Walk through qualification criteria together — confirm fit
3. Define success metrics together before agreeing to proceed
4. Set a date for the pilot agreement to be signed
5. Do not accept informal commitments — a signed LOI or pilot agreement is required before onboarding
