# Founder Pricing Notes — Final

**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Audience:** Founder only — internal pricing considerations, not for sharing with buyers  
**Purpose:** Thinking through pricing decisions before they are formalized.

---

## Current Pricing Posture

SZL Holdings has no published pricing. This is intentional at the design partner phase. Pricing is set per conversation, anchored to value delivered, not market average.

The design partner program provides the first data point for "what buyers are willing to pay" — which is more valuable than any pricing benchmark at this stage.

---

## Pricing Anchors

### Value-based anchors (what the platform saves or creates)

For each domain pack, estimate the value case:

**Aegis (security):**
- A single undetected security incident costs mid-market companies $250K–$4M (breach response, lost revenue, regulatory fines)
- A security team that runs 20% faster through governed signal-to-action is worth conservatively 0.5–1 FTE
- Value anchor: $80K–$400K/year depending on org size and incident exposure

**Vessels (maritime):**
- A single voyage anomaly caught early can prevent $50K–$500K in losses (cargo, fuel, port fees)
- A fleet operator with 10+ vessels who improves P&L visibility by 15% in the first quarter could justify $100K+/year easily
- Value anchor: $50K–$250K/year depending on fleet size

**Terra (real estate):**
- Deal evaluation cycle compression (from 4 weeks to 2 weeks) on a $50M deal pipeline is meaningful
- Missed deal signals cost 10x a platform subscription in lost transaction fees
- Value anchor: $30K–$150K/year depending on transaction volume

**PRISM Counsel (legal):**
- A litigation firm handling 200+ active matters that reduces per-matter attorney time by 2 hours/week saves ~$500K/year in realized capacity
- The compliance risk of unaudited AI outputs in legal is regulatory and reputational, not just operational
- Value anchor: $40K–$200K/year depending on firm size and matter volume

**Design partner pricing floor:**
- Any design partner price should be at minimum $20K/year annualized to represent commercial seriousness
- Design partner pricing at 30–40% discount vs. production means production should target $35K–$50K/year minimum for single-domain mid-market
- Enterprise agreements should target $100K–$500K/year for multi-domain deployments

## Pricing Traps to Avoid

### Trap 1: Pricing too low to seem accessible

A $3K/year SaaS tool signals to an enterprise buyer that this is not serious decision infrastructure. Enterprise buyers expect enterprise pricing. Under-pricing creates more credibility risk than over-pricing.

### Trap 2: Pricing per seat as the primary metric

Per-seat pricing creates a perverse incentive to limit user access, which is the opposite of adoption behavior we want. Operator seat tiering is a secondary dimension. Platform value pricing (per domain pack, per operational scope) should be the primary anchor.

### Trap 3: Offering free pilots indefinitely

Free pilots attract buyers who are not serious about commercial conversion. The design partner program has a minimum financial commitment even in the pilot phase. If a prospect refuses any financial commitment for a 90-day pilot, they are unlikely to convert to production.

### Trap 4: Publishing prices before establishing value baselines

Publishing prices before the first case studies exist means buyers compare against arbitrary market benchmarks rather than the specific value delivered. Hold pricing close until proof exists.

## Pricing Conversation Framework

When asked "what does this cost?" in an early conversation:

> "Pricing scales with domain scope and organizational size. Design partner pricing is structured to reflect early-stage risk sharing — it is at minimum 30% below what production commercial pricing will be. For a single-domain pilot with up to 10 operators, we are typically in the [range you are comfortable with] range. I'd rather anchor that to the value we define together in the pilot than quote a number before we understand what success looks like for your team."

This response:
- Does not dodge the question (gives a range if asked)
- Anchors to value rather than market comparison
- Maintains pricing leverage for the commercial conversation
- Signals that we are thinking about their specific case, not a menu

## Competitive Pricing Context

**General benchmark (enterprise SaaS in this category):**
- Mid-market point solutions: $20K–$80K/year
- Enterprise platforms: $80K–$500K/year
- SOC platforms (for Aegis comparison): $50K–$300K/year (Splunk, CrowdStrike, SentinelOne)
- Legal tech platforms: $20K–$150K/year per firm

SZL Holdings positioning is at the platform layer, not the point solution layer. Platform pricing should anchor closer to the enterprise end, even for mid-market buyers, because the multi-domain extensibility changes the ROI calculus.

---

## What to Do Before Setting Production Pricing

1. Complete at least 2 design partner pilots with documented delta metrics
2. Have at least 1 explicit conversation with each design partner about "what would you pay for this in production?"
3. Validate the pricing model against competitor comparisons — what are buyers already paying for adjacent tools?
4. Consult with any advisors or investors who have SaaS pricing expertise
5. Set production pricing before announcing it — not negotiated deal-by-deal
