# Pilot to Case Study Playbook

**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Step-by-step execution guide for converting a design partner pilot into a referenceable case study.

---

## Overview

The playbook covers three phases:

1. **Pre-Pilot Setup** (weeks –2 to 0)
2. **Pilot Execution** (weeks 1–12)
3. **Review and Capture** (weeks 12–16)

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Pilot Setup (Weeks –2 to 0)

### Week –2: Partner Qualification Confirmation

Confirm before committing resources:

- [ ] Executive sponsor named and committed
- [ ] Technical POC named and available for integration work
- [ ] Domain fit confirmed (which domain pack, which workflows)
- [ ] Pilot window agreed (60 or 90 days standard)
- [ ] Success metric agreement — what does a successful pilot look like?
- [ ] Referenceability discussion — have they agreed in principle to discuss public reference post-pilot?
- [ ] Pilot agreement signed (or LOI if agreement is in process)

### Week –1: Baseline Capture

Run baseline interview with domain practitioner and technical POC:

- [ ] Document current-state decision process (see proof-engine-final.md for template)
- [ ] Capture quantitative baseline metrics
- [ ] Capture subjective confidence scores
- [ ] List manual steps that are pilot candidates
- [ ] Agree on which metrics will be tracked during the pilot

Deliver baseline document to partner for review and sign-off. Baseline must be agreed before pilot goes live.

### Week 0: Kickoff

- [ ] Onboarding session (2–4 hours): platform orientation, role assignment, domain pack tour
- [ ] Integration setup (if applicable): API credentials, SSO configuration
- [ ] First-30-days plan reviewed and agreed (see `first-30-days-partner-plan.md`)
- [ ] Check-in cadence confirmed: weekly async (signal log + blockers), bi-weekly sync call
- [ ] Escalation path confirmed: who to contact if something breaks

---

## Phase 2: Pilot Execution (Weeks 1–12)

### Weekly (Async)

Founder sends a brief weekly check-in:

```
Week [N] Check-in — [Partner Name]

1. Top signal surfaced this week: [description or "none logged yet"]
2. Any decisions made through the platform: [yes/no + brief summary]
3. Blockers or issues: [list or "none"]
4. One thing to tune this week: [specific action]
```

Partner responds same-day or next business day. If no response within 48 hours, follow up by phone.

### Bi-Weekly (Sync Call — 30 minutes)

Agenda:
1. Signal log review — what surfaced, what mattered, what was noise (10 min)
2. Evaluation log review — AI recommendations reviewed and actioned (10 min)
3. Blockers and feature gaps (5 min)
4. Next two weeks focus (5 min)

### 30-Day Checkpoint

First formal milestone:
- [ ] Signal log reviewed — is it being maintained?
- [ ] Evaluation log reviewed — are AI recommendations being engaged?
- [ ] Baseline metrics tracked — can we start tracking deltas?
- [ ] Partner confidence check — do they feel good about the next 60 days?
- [ ] Any onboarding gaps that need addressing?
- [ ] First-30-days plan retrospective

Document and share checkpoint summary with partner within 24 hours of call.

### 60-Day Checkpoint

Mid-pilot milestone:
- [ ] Preliminary delta report — baseline vs. 60-day metrics
- [ ] Approval audit log review — are consequential actions being attributed correctly?
- [ ] Lessons captured so far
- [ ] Pilot scope check — is the original scope still the right scope?
- [ ] Begin referenceability conversation if not already started
- [ ] Early identification of case study storyline

Document and share checkpoint summary within 24 hours.

---

## Phase 3: Review and Capture (Weeks 12–16)

### 90-Day Review Meeting (60–90 minutes)

This is the primary evidence collection session. Structure it carefully.

**Agenda:**

1. Delta report presentation (15 min)
   - Walk through each baseline metric vs. pilot period metric
   - Discuss attribution confidence honestly
   - Note what changed for reasons unrelated to the platform

2. Lessons discussion (20 min)
   - What worked well? (be specific)
   - What fell short? (be direct)
   - What would you want different? (capture for roadmap)

3. Commercial conversation (15 min)
   - Are they interested in continuing?
   - What would production commitment look like?
   - What terms matter to them?

4. Referenceability conversation (10 min)
   - Are they willing to be a named reference?
   - If yes: case study, testimonial, logo, or a combination?
   - If no: can we use anonymized data?

5. Next steps (10 min)
   - Production agreement path or structured exit
   - Case study approval process if referenceable
   - Feedback loop — how do their lessons feed the roadmap?

### Post-Review: Case Study Drafting

If partner agrees to be referenceable:

1. Draft case study within 2 weeks of 90-day review
2. Share draft with partner for review (give them 5 business days)
3. Incorporate changes — they have final approval on any named content
4. Publish after explicit written approval

If partner is anonymizable only:
- Draft as industry/domain case study with no identifying information
- Still share draft for approval — even anonymized content should be reviewed

If partner declines all referenceability:
- Document lessons internally
- Use aggregate signal data (without attribution) to inform future positioning
- Respect the decision — do not pressure for reference after a no

---

## Case Study Quality Standards

Before publishing any case study:

- [ ] Partner has given explicit written approval
- [ ] All metrics are sourced from actual pilot data (no estimations without attribution confidence label)
- [ ] The case study discloses that the partner was in a design partner program (not a paying customer)
- [ ] No fabricated or rounded-up claims
- [ ] Negative observations are included — partial honesty destroys credibility faster than full honesty
- [ ] No quotes that the partner did not actually say

---

## Structured Exit (Partner Does Not Convert)

If a design partner completes the pilot without converting to production:

1. Hold a structured exit call — 30 minutes
2. Capture written feedback on why they are not converting
3. Classify the reason:
   - Product gap (feed to roadmap)
   - Commercial gap (feed to pricing/packaging)
   - Timing / internal priority (keep in pipeline)
   - Wrong fit (acknowledge and close gracefully)
4. Offer anonymized case study even in non-conversion scenarios — the lessons have value
5. Keep relationship warm — non-converting design partners often refer others
