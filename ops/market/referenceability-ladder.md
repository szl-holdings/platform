# Referenceability Ladder

**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Defines the progression from zero public proof to full named enterprise references. Each rung is a discrete milestone with specific asset outputs.

---

## The Challenge

At pre-revenue stage, the absence of public proof is expected. But buyers — especially enterprise buyers — use proof as a risk signal. The progression from "no public proof" to "named case studies" is a commercial milestone as important as any product release.

This document defines the rungs on that ladder and the specific actions required to climb each one.

---

## Current Position: Rung 0

**Status as of April 2026:** No paying customers. No public case studies. No named references.

This is the honest starting position. The goal of the design partner program is to reach Rung 3 (anonymous case studies) within the first 90 days of partner pilots, and Rung 5 (named case studies) within 6 months.

---

## The Ladder

### Rung 0: No Public Proof

**State:** Pre-design partner. Platform exists; no external validation.

**What buyers see:** Founder credibility, platform maturity signals (GitHub, API docs, architecture), and category positioning.

**Commercial risk:** High for enterprise buyers. Manageable for early adopters and design partners who understand the program structure.

**To advance to Rung 1:**
- Launch design partner program publicly
- Set expectations correctly: partners are co-validators, not paying customers yet

---

### Rung 1: Design Partner Program Announcement

**State:** Design partner program announced publicly with defined terms.

**What buyers see:** A structured path to first commercial relationships. The program is evidence of commercial seriousness even before pilots complete.

**Assets to create:**
- Public design partner program page or section on /platform
- Design partner offer document available for download or review
- Clear statement of what partners get and what they commit

**Commercial risk:** Reduces risk slightly — shows the commercial motion is structured.

**To advance to Rung 2:**
- Secure first design partner commitment
- Announce partnership (with partner permission) or note "first design partner onboarded" publicly

---

### Rung 2: First Design Partner Onboarded

**State:** At least one design partner is actively using the platform.

**What buyers see:** Someone besides the founder believes this is worth exploring.

**Assets to create:**
- Brief public announcement (blog post, LinkedIn): "We've onboarded our first design partner in [domain]"
- No specifics without partner permission
- Optional: partner quote in announcement if they agree

**Commercial risk:** Moderate. Buyers can see validation is happening.

**To advance to Rung 3:**
- Complete first pilot
- Capture delta data with partner consent
- Write anonymous case study

---

### Rung 3: Anonymous Case Study Published

**State:** First completed pilot with documented delta metrics. Partner agreed to anonymized case study.

**What buyers see:** Real proof of operational impact, even without named attribution.

**Assets to create:**
- Anonymous case study: "[Industry] Firm Reduces Decision Cycle Time by X% Using Governed Decision Infrastructure"
- Published on /case-studies or equivalent page
- Available for sharing in diligence

**Requirements:**
- Metrics sourced from actual pilot data
- Attribution confidence stated for each metric
- Partner approved the anonymized version

**Commercial risk:** Meaningfully reduced. Buyers can see an outcome story.

**To advance to Rung 4:**
- Get partner agreement to be identified by industry/size, if not name
- Add testimonial if partner will provide a quote

---

### Rung 4: Testimonial with Attribution

**State:** At least one partner willing to provide a named quote.

**What buyers see:** A real person, at a real organization, saying something specific about their experience.

**Testimonial requirements:**
- Real quote from real person (never fabricated)
- Name and title required; company name if they agree
- Specific to what they experienced (not generic "it's great")
- Reviewed and approved in writing by the person quoted

**Example of acceptable testimonial:**
> "The Proof Chain audit trail gave us something we'd been trying to build ourselves for two years — a way to show our board exactly what decision was made, by whom, based on what evidence, and what happened next."
> — Head of Operations, [Industry] Company

**Example of unacceptable testimonial:**
> "This platform is game-changing and transforms our operations."

**To advance to Rung 5:**
- Get partner agreement to be named in a full case study
- Deliver named case study within 30 days of agreement

---

### Rung 5: Named Case Study Published

**State:** Named, attributed case study with specific metrics, quotes, and company identification.

**What buyers see:** Full external validation — a named organization willing to stake their reputation on their experience.

**Case study requirements:**
- Company name (or parent entity name)
- Partner contact name and title
- Specific delta metrics with attribution confidence
- Direct quotes
- Honest inclusion of any gaps or limitations observed
- Partner approved in writing before publication

**Commercial impact:** This is the milestone that materially changes enterprise buyer risk perception. One named case study from a credible organization changes the commercial conversation.

---

### Rung 6: Multiple Named References Across Domains

**State:** At least 2-3 named case studies across different domain packs.

**What buyers see:** Pattern, not anomaly. Cross-domain proof that the platform delivers in multiple contexts.

**Commercial impact:** Enables analysts, advisors, and procurement to verify independently. Enables peer reference calls.

---

### Rung 7: Peer Reference Calls Available

**State:** Partners willing to speak with prospective buyers on the phone.

**What buyers see:** Maximum trust signal — a real person willing to answer unscripted questions.

**To enable peer reference calls:**
- Get explicit partner agreement (some will agree to case study but not live calls)
- Prepare partners with what to expect
- Brief the prospective buyer on appropriate scope of the call
