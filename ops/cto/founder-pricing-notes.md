## Context

These are working pricing notes for the founding team. They are intentionally conservative — designed for the design-partner stage where speed of commitment matters more than margin optimization. All numbers here are starting points for negotiation, not published prices.

The goal at this stage is to:
1. Convert design partners to paid before the market understands the value
2. Lock first-mover pricing that feels fair and keeps partners loyal during expansion
3. Build a pricing intuition from real conversations before committing to a model

## Pricing Orientation

We are not a SaaS app. The right pricing comparables are:

| Comparable | Why It Matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| Enterprise middleware (MuleSoft, Boomi) | $50K–$500K ARR; integration-heavy buyers expect annual contracts |
| Operational intelligence (Palantir AIP, C3.ai) | $200K–$2M ARR; domain specificity commands premium |
| Legal tech enterprise (Relativity, Casetext enterprise) | $50K–$300K ARR; matter-based or seat-based |
| GRC / compliance platforms (ServiceNow, Archer) | $100K–$500K ARR; annual, procurement-driven |

We should not price against point tools (Asana, Monday, Notion). If a prospect compares us to those, the qualification process failed.

## Pilot-to-Paid Conversion Notes

When a design partner is ready to convert:

1. **Anchor to value delivered, not to the rack rate.** Start the conversation with: "In the pilot, we surfaced [N] issues and routed [N] actions. What's that worth to your team per year?"
2. **Let them propose a number first.** Founders habitually overprice or underprice. Get their number on the table before you anchor.
3. **Lock the term.** 12-month minimum. 24-month preferred, with the first-mover discount as the incentive.
4. **Never discount on scope — only on term.** If they push back on price, offer a longer commitment at a lower rate. Do not remove features or domain packs from the deal.
5. **Payment terms:** Annual upfront preferred. Net-60 is the maximum. Quarterly billing is available for Operator+ tier only.

## Common Negotiation Scenarios

| Scenario | Recommended Response |
|----------|---------------------|
| "Can we do monthly billing?" | "We don't offer monthly for enterprise contracts. Annual is standard. We can do a 12-month with Net-30." |
| "We need to start smaller" | "Let's define what 'smaller' means. Fewer users, fewer connectors, or a shorter initial term?" |
| "Our procurement needs a SOW, not a SaaS agreement" | "We can do a services-wrapped contract structure. Let's get legal involved." |
| "Can we extend the pilot instead of committing?" | "We can do a 30-day extension once. After that, we ask for a commitment or close cleanly." |
| "What does your pricing look like in year 2?" | "Contracts include a 5% annual uplift cap. Your first-mover rate is the base." |
| "We need to compare your pricing to [competitor]" | "Happy to help you build a fair comparison. What's the specific capability you're evaluating?" |

## What to Never Do in a Pricing Conversation

- Do not email a number. Have the conversation first.
- Do not discount without a concession. Every discount should buy something — longer term, larger seat count, case study rights, or public reference.
- Do not mention "freemium" or "self-serve." It reframes the buyer's mental model incorrectly.
- Do not let the conversation get stuck on per-seat math. Enterprise buyers want total value, not per-user arithmetic.
- Do not offer to "check with the team" on price. Founders have pricing authority. Own it.
