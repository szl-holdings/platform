## What We Built

SZL Holdings is a governed decision infrastructure platform — the infrastructure layer that connects business signals to confirmed, accountable action across complex enterprises.

Over the past 18 months, I built five domain-specific platforms on a single shared architecture: Lyte (business observability), Aegis (defense and intelligence), Vessels (maritime), Terra (real estate), and Carlota Jo (advisory). Every platform shares one execution fabric (Alloy), one design system, one authentication model, and one data layer.

This is not a portfolio of disconnected products. It is a compounding system. Each vertical strengthens the shared entity graph, enriches the cross-domain intelligence layer, and compounds the value of the shared infrastructure investment.

---

## Why I Built It This Way

The enterprise software market has a structural problem: tools that observe, tools that analyze, and tools that execute are all built separately and expected to talk to each other. They mostly don't.

The result is that the people responsible for outcomes — operations leaders, compliance officers, fleet executives, security directors — spend most of their time synthesizing fragmented signals manually. That's expensive, slow, and error-prone.

The insight is not that these organizations need more data. They have too much data. They need a platform that reasons across their data, routes insight to the right human for confirmation, and records the decision with complete provenance.

That platform needs to be architectural from the start — not assembled after the fact. That's why SZL Holdings was designed as a holding company: one architecture, multiple markets, one shared investment in the backbone.

## What the Platform Proves

**It can be built.** Five production-grade platforms on one shared architecture, built by a single engineer, demonstrating that the compounding design thesis works in practice. The proof of architectural leverage is in the platform itself.

**The governance model is structural.** Alloy's human-in-the-loop approval gates are not a feature — they are enforced at the workflow engine level. AI agents advise; humans confirm; the proof chain records everything. This is not an aspiration; it is the code.

**The verticals are real.** Terra pulls live distress data from NYC Open Data. Aegis pulls live CVEs from NVD and live KEVs from CISA. Vessels models real voyage economics. Lyte's PRISM framework models real business process friction. None of this is a mockup.

**The architecture scales.** Adding a new vertical requires: a domain-specific observation layer, a domain-tuned AI agent, and domain-specific action playbooks. Alloy, the design system, authentication, and the database backbone are already built and do not need to be rebuilt.

## The Market Opportunity

Business Observability is a category that does not have a dominant platform. The tools that come closest — Datadog (infrastructure), Splunk (security analytics), Palantir (government analytics) — serve specific verticals without the cross-domain architecture that Business Observability requires.

The total addressable market across the five verticals SZL currently serves — business operations software, cybersecurity, maritime intelligence, commercial real estate, and premium advisory — is measured in the hundreds of billions. The platform's expansion logic (one new Observe layer per vertical; all other infrastructure shared) means the marginal cost of entering a new market compresses with every vertical added.
