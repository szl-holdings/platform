## Why a Design Partner Program

We are creating a category. The buyers we want do not have a budget line for "governed decision infrastructure" yet. We need:

1. **Proof points** — real organizations using the loop on real data
2. **Reference customers** — willing to talk to other prospects
3. **Product input** — operators co-shaping the platform with us
4. **Case studies** — the artifacts that turn category claim into category fact
5. **Logo wall** — credibility for the next dozen buyers

The design partner program is how we get all five at the same time.

---

## Who Qualifies

A design partner profile:

| Trait | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| Operator-led decision | The user (not procurement) is the champion |
| 50–500-person organization | Small enough to move fast; large enough to matter |
| One clear domain pain | Aegis, Vessels, Terra, PRISM Counsel, or Carlota Jo as primary |
| At least one regulated decision type | Audit and governance are real requirements, not "nice-to-have" |
| Willing to share a case study (anonymized OK) | Without this, they are a customer, not a partner |
| Willing to take a reference call (≤ 4 per year) | The currency we are buying with the discount |
| Founder or operator-level sponsor on their side | Decisions made in days, not quarters |

A profile that **does not qualify**:

- Procurement-driven evaluation (RFP-style) — these belong in the standard sales motion
- "We want to evaluate" with no defined success metric
- No willingness to be referenced
- > 1000 person organization with multi-quarter procurement cycles (better fit for Enterprise sales)

## Program Slots

We hold the program to a small, named cohort to keep relationships dense and feedback usable.

| Cohort | Slots | Domain mix | Status |
|--------|-------|-----------|--------|
| Cohort 1 (Q2 2026) | 6 | 2 Aegis · 2 Vessels · 1 Terra · 1 PRISM Counsel | Open |
| Cohort 2 (Q4 2026) | 6 | TBD based on Cohort 1 outcomes | Closed |
| Cohort 3 (Q2 2027) | 6 | TBD | Closed |

A maximum of 18 design partners across the first three cohorts. After Cohort 3 we transition to standard commercial sales.

## What Partners Commit To

| Commitment | Detail |
|------------|--------|
| Real production deployment | Not a sandbox; real signals, real decisions |
| Defined success metric | Co-defined with us before kickoff (see [PROOF_OF_VALUE_PLAYBOOK.md](PROOF_OF_VALUE_PLAYBOOK.md)) |
| Quarterly product feedback session | Founder + their operator champion |
| Reference calls (up to 4 per year) | With prospects we ask them to speak with |
| Case study (one per year) | Anonymized acceptable; named preferred |
| Logo permission | Use of their logo on our site (their veto rights honored) |
| Honest feedback | Including when something is broken or wrong |
| Public reference at one event per year | Conference panel, podcast, or written piece |

If a partner cannot meet these, they do not belong in the program — but they may belong in the standard commercial relationship at full pricing.

## Program Structure

### Stage 1: Selection (2–4 weeks)

| Step | Owner |
|------|-------|
| Founder intro call | Founder |
| Operator demo | Demo operator |
| Technical demo | Demo operator |
| Success metric scoping | Founder + operator champion |
| Diligence packet review | Customer's technical lead |
| MSA signature | Customer's legal + founder |

Decision: in or out. Time-bound. We do not chase indecision.

### Stage 2: Onboarding (2–4 weeks)

| Step | Owner |
|------|-------|
| Tenant provisioning | DevOps |
| Identity setup (OIDC/SCIM) | DevOps + customer IT |
| Domain pack configuration | CSM + customer operator |
| Signal source connection | Customer + SZL integration support |
| Operator training (1 cohort) | CSM |
| Success metric instrumentation | CSM + customer operator |

### Stage 3: Active Pilot (90 days)

| Cadence | Activity | Owner |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Weekly (first 4 weeks) | Operator check-in | CSM |
| Biweekly (weeks 5–12) | Operator check-in | CSM |
| Monthly | Founder check-in | Founder |
| End of pilot | Success metric review | Founder + customer champion |
| End of pilot | Decision: convert / extend / exit | Both parties |

The pilot is structured by [PILOT_PLAYBOOK.md](PILOT_PLAYBOOK.md) and measured by [DESIGN_PARTNER_SCORECARD.md](DESIGN_PARTNER_SCORECARD.md).

### Stage 4: Commercial Conversion

If success metric is met: convert to standard commercial relationship at design partner pricing.

If not met: structured exit. Customer keeps audit data; we keep what we learned. No hard feelings — we want to know when the platform does not fit.

## Exit Mechanics

A design partner can exit the program for any reason at end of pilot or end of term. We commit to:

- 90-day data export window (per [DATA-RETENTION.md](DATA-RETENTION.md))
- No claw-back of pilot pricing
- No NDA constraints on their honest assessment of why they exited
- Exit interview with founder

We may exit a partner relationship if:

- Commitments are not met (case study, reference, feedback)
- Their use of the platform falls below 25% of expected (the partnership is not real)
- Reputational concerns emerge

---

## Why This Is the Right Motion Now

Design partners are the right motion when:

- The category is new (true here)
- The price point requires conviction, not procurement (true here)
- The product needs operator co-design (true here)
- Reference customers compound (true here)

Design partners are the wrong motion when:

- The category is mature (not us)
- The product is a commodity (not us)
- Procurement is the buyer (not us in 2026; possibly in 2028)
- Logos are the only goal (we want feedback as much as logos)

We are precisely in the design-partner moment.
